# Voice Discipline — x-lead-qualifier

The DM-specific voice rules. Different from the
post-focused voice (more conversational, longer, no
280-char limit, no post-banned-phrase list). The
Scribe's task spec enforces these as a hard rule.

## DM-specific voice rules

### Staccato periods (the load-bearing element)

Same as posts: short sentences with periods, no
run-on sentences. The lead reads the DM on their
phone; staccato is readable in 5 seconds.

**Bad:** "I noticed your post about CRM automation and
I think the way most teams handle this is that they
spend a lot of time on manual data entry which is
really inefficient."

**Good:** "Saw your post about CRM automation. The
manual data entry is the bottleneck. 6 hours/week
per rep."

### Lead with a punch (the first sentence)

The first sentence lands the hardest fact. The lead
decides in 3 seconds whether to read the rest.

**Bad:** "Hi, hope you're doing well! I saw your post
and wanted to reach out about..."

**Good:** "Saw your post about the 6 hours/week on
manual data entry. That's the exact failure I see
in 80% of CRM implementations."

### Specific numbers (the unit economics)

DM-specific: the lead is a peer, not a follower. Use
real numbers (cost, time, percentage). Don't be vague.

**Bad:** "This can save you a lot of time and money."

**Good:** "Quick tip: 6 hours/week × $50/hr fully
loaded = $15,600/year per rep. If you have 3 reps,
that's $46,800/year."

### Conversational (talks TO, not AT)

DM-specific: the lead is one person, not an audience.
Use "you" and "I" frequently. Don't use "we" or
"users."

**Bad:** "Users often find that automation can help
in this area."

**Good:** "If your reps are spending 6 hours/week
on manual data entry, you're leaving $15K/year on
the table per rep."

### No emoji (default)

DMs default to no emoji. The lead may use emoji; the
Scribe mirrors the lead's tone (1-2 emoji if the lead
used them). Default = no emoji.

**Bad:** "🎉 Awesome post! 🚀 Here's a tip..."

**Good:** "Saw your post. Here's a tip: ..."

### No "DM me" (except the CTA)

The Scribe's draft must NOT use "DM me" or "follow
for more" or "link in bio" — except the specific
qualification CTA, which IS allowed.

The CTA is the only "DM me" in the draft:

> "If you want me to look at your current stack for
> free, DM me 'AUDIT' and I'll spend 15 minutes on
> it."

## DM-specific banned phrases

These are banned in DMs (different from the post
banned list):

- "dive into" (overused, no information)
- "delve into" (overused, no information)
- "unlock" (vague, no information)
- "game-changer" (overused)
- "harness the power of" (corporate fluff)
- "in today's fast-paced world" (LinkedIn-ese)
- "at the end of the day" (filler)
- "let's unpack this" (overused, condescending)

The post banned-phrase list (dive into / delve into /
paradigm shift / seamlessly / etc.) is enforced by
the Scribe's persona spec. The DM banned list is
in addition to the post list.

## Voice match check (the Scribe's verification)

The Scribe self-checks:
- Staccato periods (sentence count: ~3-5 in a 200-
  word DM)
- Lead with a punch (first sentence has the strongest
  fact)
- Specific numbers (at least one real number in the
  tip section)
- Conversational (you/I > we/users)
- No emoji (default; mirror if lead used)
- No "DM me" except the CTA

If any check fails, the Scribe halts and surfaces the
draft for the chief to review.

## What this voice discipline is NOT

- **Not the post voice.** Posts are broadcast (many
  readers, terse, 280 chars). DMs are 1:1 (one
  reader, conversational, longer).
- **Not a sales pitch.** The DM is the first step of a
  sales funnel, not the pitch. The technical tip is
  the value.
- **Not a templated response.** The Scribe fills in
  the template with source-specific details, not a
  copy-paste of the template.
- **Not universal.** Different intent types may have
  different voice patterns. A `pain_point_statement`
  DM is more empathetic; an `ai_automation_question`
  DM is more technical. The Scribe adapts.
