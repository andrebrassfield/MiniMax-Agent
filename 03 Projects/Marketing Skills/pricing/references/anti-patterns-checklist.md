# Pricing Anti-Patterns Checklist

The full banned-moves list for pricing. Pricing that ships with any of these is weaker, not stronger. Run this list before the price goes live.

---

## 1. Unanchored pricing

**What it looks like:** "$5,000" alone with no reference price.

**Why it's banned:** Any price without an anchor reads as expensive. The buyer has nothing to compare against.

**Replacement:** Always anchor. Use alternative cost, total value, or future cost of inaction (see `anchor-analysis.md`).

---

## 2. Fake prior price

**What it looks like:** "Was $999, NOW $499" with no actual $999 history. Or "List price $1,500, our price $497" without the list being a real list price.

**Why it's banned:** Buyers can check. The FTC has rules about this. Once buyers catch it, every other claim on the page loses credibility.

**Replacement:** Real anchor (alternative cost) or genuine early-bird pricing with a clear deadline and visible history.

---

## 3. Charm pricing at high ticket

**What it looks like:** $9,997 instead of $10,000 for a premium offer. $24,997 instead of $25,000 for consulting.

**Why it's banned:** At high ticket, charm pricing reads as cheap or as hiding the round number. It signals the operator isn't confident in the price.

**Replacement:** Round numbers at $500+. $10,000, $25,000, $50,000. Charm pricing only below $500.

---

## 4. Transparently bad decoy tier

**What it looks like:** A 3rd tier that's obviously stripped down or overpriced to make Tier 2 look good. "Enterprise: $5,000 for one 30-min call."

**Why it's banned:** Buyers see through it. The decoy makes Tier 2 feel like a manipulation, not a value.

**Replacement:** Make the premium tier a real offer that some buyers will actually choose. See `tiered-pricing.md`.

---

## 5. "Contact us for pricing" on offer surface

**What it looks like:** B2B or premium service with no published price. Buyer has to talk to sales to learn the cost.

**Why it's banned:** Without an anchor, "contact us" reads as "this is expensive and we don't want to show you." Conversion-killing.

**Replacement:** Show the price on the offer surface, with an anchor. "Starting at $25,000 for a 90-day engagement."

---

## 6. No premium for full pay

**What it looks like:** "$5,000 or 6 × $833" with no difference. Or $5,000 vs 6 × $900 (multi-pay costs MORE).

**Why it's banned:** Without a discount for full pay, every buyer picks multi-pay. You lose the lump-sum option and the cashflow benefit.

**Replacement:** 5–15% discount for paying in full. See `payment-structure-math.md`.

---

## 7. Price without payment structure

**What it looks like:** "$5,000" with no installment option. Forces lump-sum or buyer leaves.

**Why it's banned:** Many buyers can't pay lump-sum at mid-to-high ticket. Without installments, you lose them.

**Replacement:** Always show payment structure on the offer surface. "$5,000 or 3 × $1,750 (save $250 with full pay)."

---

## 8. Discounting without justification

**What it looks like:** Random "20% off this week!" with no reason. Or ongoing "introductory pricing" forever.

**Why it's banned:** Random discounting trains buyers to wait. They learn the price will drop if they're patient.

**Replacement:** Justified discounts only — early-bird with real deadline, launch-week pricing, or new-customer incentive with clear terms.

---

## 9. Inflated total value

**What it looks like:** "Total value: $5,000, yours for $497" with values that wouldn't survive scrutiny.

**Why it's banned:** Buyers do the math. Inflated values train them to distrust the offer.

**Replacement:** Real or honestly perceived value per component. If you can't defend it, don't state a number. See `/offers` references/bonus-stacking.md.

---

## 10. Hidden fees

**What it looks like:** Price on the page is $497. Checkout shows $547 with "service fees" or "platform fees."

**Why it's banned:** Hidden fees kill conversion. Buyers feel tricked.

**Replacement:** Show total price upfront. If there are taxes or fees, show them on the offer surface, not at checkout.

---

## 11. Starting-at pricing that's misleading

**What it looks like:** "Starting at $99" when most buyers pay $1,000.

**Why it's banned:** Misleading. Buyers find out and feel tricked.

**Replacement:** Either show the typical price, or clearly state the range. "From $99 (DIY) to $1,500 (1:1 coaching)."

---

## 12. Stacked anchors that compete

**What it looks like:** Alternative cost + total value + future cost of inaction + competitor comparison, all on the same page.

**Why it's banned:** Too many anchors confuse the buyer. They get distrustful.

**Replacement:** One primary anchor. A secondary anchor only if it's genuinely true and reinforces the primary.

---

## 13. Price that doesn't match the offer

**What it looks like:** A complex offer with multiple deliverables priced at $97. Or a simple info product priced at $5,000.

**Why it's banned:** Price doesn't match offer depth. Buyer feels either ripped off or that the operator is overcharging.

**Replacement:** Price matches offer depth. Run through `/offers` first if price feels off.

---

## 14. Price changing without communication

**What it looks like:** Buyer saw $497 yesterday, today it's $697, no explanation.

**Why it's banned:** Surprises erode trust. Buyers who almost bought yesterday feel punished.

**Replacement:** Communicate price changes. "Launch price $497 until Friday. Then $697." Or grandfather existing buyers.

---

## 15. Premium pricing without premium feel

**What it looks like:** $10,000 price on a basic-looking page with no proof, no guarantee, no operator credibility.

**Why it's banned:** High prices need matching presentation. Without it, the price feels arbitrary or exploitative.

**Replacement:** Premium pricing needs premium surface — design, copy, proof, guarantee, operator credibility. Match the price to the page.

---

## Quick checklist (use before shipping pricing)

Before the price goes live, every item must be NO:

- [ ] No unanchored prices
- [ ] No fake prior prices
- [ ] No charm pricing at high ticket
- [ ] No transparently bad decoy tier
- [ ] No "contact us" without an anchor
- [ ] No multi-pay without premium for full pay
- [ ] No price without payment structure
- [ ] No discounting without justification
- [ ] No inflated total value
- [ ] No hidden fees
- [ ] No misleading "starting at" pricing
- [ ] No stacked competing anchors
- [ ] No price that doesn't match the offer
- [ ] No price changes without communication
- [ ] No premium pricing without premium feel

If any item is YES, fix before shipping. Pricing anti-patterns erode trust faster than almost any other mistake.
