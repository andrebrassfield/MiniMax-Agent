# Payment Structure Math

The price isn't just the number — it's how the buyer pays. The right structure can lift conversion 30–50% by matching buyer cashflow. The wrong structure leaves money on the table.

---

## The 6 structures

### 1. One-Time Lump-Sum

**What:** Buyer pays full amount in one transaction.

**When it works:**
- Low-ticket ($50–$500) — installment friction exceeds installment benefit.
- Buyer has the cash (B2B, recent liquidity event, etc.).
- High-ticket where you want to filter for commitment.

**Conversion impact:** Lower than multi-pay at high ticket (lump-sum is harder). Higher at low ticket.

**Margin impact:** Best. No transaction fees on installments. Cash immediately.

---

### 2. Multi-Pay (Installments)

**What:** Buyer pays in 2–6 installments, usually monthly.

**When it works:**
- Mid-to-high ticket ($1k–$10k) where lump-sum blocks buyers.
- Buyer has steady cashflow (employed, recurring revenue).
- You want to lift conversion without lowering the headline price.

**Conversion impact:** 30–80% lift over lump-sum at $1k+. Diminishing returns at higher installments (4+ installments).

**Margin impact:** Lower per-buyer revenue if you discount for full pay. Payment processor fees on each transaction.

**Common patterns:**
- 2-pay: 50/50 (start/end) or 60/40 (heavier upfront)
- 3-pay: 33/33/33 or 40/30/30
- 4-pay: 25% monthly
- 6-pay: monthly through fulfillment period

---

### 3. Monthly Recurring (Retainer / Subscription)

**What:** Buyer pays monthly, ongoing. Cancels when they stop.

**When it works:**
- Ongoing service (agency retainer, coaching subscription, software).
- Buyer wants flexibility to exit.
- You have predictable fulfillment cost per month.

**Conversion impact:** Higher than annual at same total price (monthly feels smaller).

**Margin impact:** Recurring revenue compounds. High LTV if retention is good. Bad if churn is high.

**Common patterns:**
- Monthly: $X/mo, cancel anytime
- Annual: $Y (saves Z%) — locks in revenue

---

### 4. Milestone Payments

**What:** Buyer pays at specific milestones in the engagement.

**When it works:**
- Project-based services (50% start, 50% delivery).
- B2B engagements with phase gates.
- High-ticket consulting where you want shared risk.

**Conversion impact:** Lower than monthly recurring (buyer has to commit to milestones). Higher than full upfront (less cash risk).

**Margin impact:** Cash flow tied to milestones. Risk if buyer cancels mid-project.

**Common patterns:**
- 50/50: half on signing, half on delivery
- 33/33/33: kickoff / midpoint / delivery
- 30/40/30: signing / midpoint / delivery (heavier midpoint)

---

### 5. Performance / Hybrid

**What:** Base fee + variable component tied to results.

**When it works:**
- Lead gen / paid acquisition agencies.
- High-confidence operators willing to bet on outcomes.
- B2B engagements where buyer wants alignment.

**Conversion impact:** Higher (alignment reduces buyer risk). Operator risk is real.

**Margin impact:** Variable. Base covers costs; upside is the bonus. Operator downside if performance is bad.

**Common patterns:**
- Base + % of revenue: e.g., $3k/mo + 5% of new MRR
- Base + per-result: e.g., $2k/mo + $X per qualified lead
- Pure performance: only % (rare — operator needs very high confidence)

---

### 6. Custom / Proposal

**What:** Price negotiated per engagement after discovery.

**When it works:**
- Enterprise B2B ($25k+).
- Custom engagements where scope is variable.
- High-ticket consulting where the buyer needs to feel co-designed.

**Conversion impact:** Lower per-opportunity (more friction). Higher per-buyer when it closes.

**Margin impact:** Variable. Custom pricing requires custom scoping.

**Common patterns:**
- Tiered proposal: 3 options at 3 price points
- Hourly + cap: $X/hr with a ceiling
- Fixed bid: after discovery, single fixed price
- Value-based: priced against ROI, not hours

## The premium-for-full-pay rule

When offering multi-pay, give a discount for paying in full.

**Standard discount: 5–15%.**

Why:
- Less than 5%: no one pays lump-sum. You lose the cashflow benefit.
- More than 15%: everyone pays lump-sum. Multi-pay option is theater.

**Worked example:**

Multi-pay: 3 × $2,750 = $8,250 total.
Premium for full pay (10% discount): $7,500 lump-sum.

Buyer pays $750 less for full pay. You get cash immediately, save on transaction fees, and filter for higher-commitment buyers.

**Don't give >15% — it cannibalizes multi-pay.**

## Buyer-cashflow matching

The right structure depends on the buyer's cashflow.

| Buyer | Best structure |
|-------|----------------|
| B2C individual, employed | Monthly recurring or multi-pay |
| B2C individual, business owner | Lump-sum or multi-pay |
| B2B (SMB, <50 employees) | Multi-pay or monthly retainer |
| B2B (mid-market) | Monthly retainer or milestone |
| B2B (enterprise) | Custom / milestone |
| SaaS / subscription | Monthly recurring |

**Default rule:** when in doubt, monthly recurring or 3-pay. Matches most buyer cashflows.

## Math: which structure maximizes revenue?

The math is straightforward but rarely done.

**Inputs:**
- Conversion rate at each structure
- Average transaction value at each structure
- Fulfillment cost per buyer
- Payment processor fees
- Refund rate per structure

**Example:**

| Structure | Conv | ATV | Rev/visitor | Margin/buyer |
|-----------|------|-----|-------------|--------------|
| Lump-sum $7,500 | 3% | $7,500 | $225 | $5,500 |
| 3-pay $2,750 | 5% | $8,250 | $413 | $5,250 (after processor fees) |
| 6-pay $1,400 | 6% | $8,400 | $504 | $5,150 |

Multi-pay wins on revenue/visitor despite lower margin/buyer, because conversion lifts more than margin falls.

But: if refund rate is higher on multi-pay (which it often is — buyer feels less committed), the math shifts. Track refund rate by structure.

## Payment structure anti-patterns

### Multi-pay with no full-pay premium
Everyone picks multi-pay. You lose the lump-sum option. Always offer a discount for full pay.

### Monthly recurring on a project
Buyer pays monthly but you've already delivered. Use milestone or lump-sum for projects.

### Custom pricing on low-ticket
Custom pricing on $200 offers is friction without justification. Use a published price.

### Multi-pay with too many installments
6+ installments on a short program (4 weeks) creates payment-without-engagement. Buyer drops off, stops paying. Use 2–3 installments.

### Long recurring with no exit
Buyer can't cancel or exit. High churn pressure, refund disputes. Always have a clear exit.

---

## When to revisit structure

- Conversion is dropping despite stable traffic → test new structure.
- Refund rate is climbing → check if multi-pay buyers are less committed.
- Buyer complaints mention cashflow → add or restructure payment plan.
- Annual churn is high on recurring → add an annual discount to lock in.
