# Copywriting Anti-Patterns Checklist

The full banned-moves list for copywriting. Copy that ships with any of these is weaker, not stronger. Run this list before the copy goes live.

---

## 1. Banned hype words

**The full banned list:**
- `game-changing`
- `revolutionary`
- `secret`
- `secret method`
- `secret sauce`
- `magic`
- `breakthrough`
- `unlock`
- `unleash`
- `transformational`
- `life-changing`
- `mind-blowing`
- `unbelievable`
- `one weird trick`
- `they don't want you to know`

**Why they're banned:** Overused. Buyers have read them too many times. They signal "this offer doesn't have substance."

**Replacement:** Specifics. "Add $40k MRR in 90 days" beats "revolutionary growth system."

---

## 2. Fake urgency

**What it looks like:** "24 hours left!" with no real deadline. "Today only!" repeated weekly.

**Why it's banned:** Buyers can tell. Manufactured urgency trains them to ignore future urgency.

**Replacement:** Real urgency from `/launch` references/scarcity-frameworks.md. Cohort caps, calendar windows, asset limits.

---

## 3. Corporate sludge

**The full banned list:**
- `synergy` / `synergies`
- `leverage` (as verb)
- `value-add` / `value-added`
- `solutions-driven`
- `in today's fast-paced world`
- `at the end of the day`
- `when all is said and done`
- `it's important to note`
- `going forward`
- `circle back`
- `touch base`
- `unpack` (as verb)

**Why it's banned:** Corporate filler. Says nothing. Buyers tune out.

**Replacement:** State the thing. Don't soften it.

---

## 4. Generic testimonials

**What it looks like:** "This changed my life!" with no specifics. "Amazing product!" with no context.

**Why it's banned:** Generic testimonials don't move Perceived Likelihood. Buyers need specifics to mirror their own situation.

**Replacement:**
- Name + role + company
- Before state
- What they did
- Specific result
- Optional quote

---

## 5. Fake scarcity

**What it looks like:** "Only 3 spots left!" with no actual cap. "Limited spots available" with no specifics.

**Why it's banned:** Same as fake urgency. Once buyers find out, all scarcity claims collapse.

**Replacement:** Real scarcity (see `/launch`).

---

## 6. Buried CTAs

**What it looks like:** The offer is buried below 3,000 words of setup. The CTA button is at the very bottom with no preview.

**Why it's banned:** Most buyers don't scroll to the bottom. The CTA must be visible above and within the page.

**Replacement:** CTA above the fold (smaller), mid-page (medium), bottom (primary). Repeat the offer near each CTA.

---

## 7. Unanchored prices

**What it looks like:** "$5,000" alone on the page with no reference.

**Why it's banned:** Unanchored prices read as expensive. See `/pricing` references.

**Replacement:** Anchor the price (alternative cost, total value, future cost of inaction).

---

## 8. Fake authority

**What it looks like:** "As seen in Forbes" without logo rights. "Featured by TechCrunch" with no actual feature. Stock-photo headshots next to fake quotes.

**Why it's banned:** Made-up authority is fraud. Even when buyers don't catch it, it kills trust for the ones who do.

**Replacement:** Real press with real logos (with permission). Real testimonials with real names. No fake proof.

---

## 9. Mismatch between surface and offer

**What it looks like:** A $5,000 offer on a long-form sales page written for a $47 ebook. Or a $47 ebook on a long-form sales page written for $5,000.

**Why it's banned:** Surface should match offer price and complexity. Low-ticket surfaces don't earn long-form. High-ticket offers don't earn short-form.

**Replacement:** Match surface to offer. See `/offers` references/formats-by-business-type.md.

---

## 10. False scarcity in headlines

**What it looks like:** "Last chance to..." in a headline with no real deadline. "Today only" repeated.

**Why it's banned:** Same as fake urgency. Headlines that lie erode brand trust.

**Replacement:** Real deadlines stated specifically. "Cart closes Friday at 5pm ET."

---

## 11. Headline + body mismatch

**What it looks like:** Headline promises "Add $40k MRR in 90 days." Body talks about mindset, productivity, and generic business growth.

**Why it's banned:** Buyers feel bait-and-switched. The headline sets an expectation the body doesn't deliver.

**Replacement:** Headline, sub-headline, and body all reinforce the same promise.

---

## 12. Pop-up on entry

**What it looks like:** Email capture pop-up before the buyer has read any content.

**Why it's banned:** High bounce rate. The buyer came for the offer, not the email list.

**Replacement:** Scroll-triggered or exit-intent pop-up. Let the buyer see the offer first.

---

## 13. "Click here" CTAs

**What it looks like:** "Click here to learn more." "Submit." "Sign up."

**Why it's banned:** Generic CTAs don't tell the buyer what they get. Loses 10–30% of conversion vs specific copy.

**Replacement:** First-person, specific. "Yes, I want to add $40k MRR — $7,500."

---

## 14. Burying the price

**What it looks like:** Buyer has to scroll through 2,000 words to find the price. Or price is hidden behind "Contact us."

**Why it's banned:** Buyers expect to see the price. Hiding it signals "expensive."

**Replacement:** Price on the offer surface, anchored.

---

## 15. "I'm not for you" framing that's actually for everyone

**What it looks like:** "This isn't for everyone... [but actually, it is]."

**Why it's banned:** Fake exclusivity reads as manipulation. Buyers see through it.

**Replacement:** Real specificity. "This is for B2B SaaS founders at $1k–$10k MRR. If you're pre-revenue or above $50k MRR, this isn't for you."

---

## 16. Hidden fees

**What it looks like:** Page says $497. Checkout shows $547 with "service fees."

**Why it's banned:** Hidden fees kill conversion. Buyers feel tricked.

**Replacement:** Show total price upfront. If taxes or fees apply, show them on the offer surface.

---

## 17. Bonus inflation in copy

**What it looks like:** "Total value $11,458, yours for $497" with bonus values that wouldn't survive scrutiny.

**Why it's banned:** Same as `/pricing` anti-patterns. Inflated values train buyers to distrust the offer.

**Replacement:** Real or honestly perceived value per bonus. See `/offers` references/bonus-stacking.md.

---

## 18. Repetition as emphasis

**What it looks like:** Saying the same thing three times in slightly different ways to "drive it home."

**Why it's banned:** Buyers feel patronized. Once is enough; twice is a stretch.

**Replacement:** State the thing once, clearly. Move on.

---

## 19. Copy that doesn't match the buyer

**What it looks like:** Writing for "entrepreneurs" when the buyer is a B2B SaaS founder with specific MRR. Writing for "small businesses" when the buyer is a 50-person company.

**Why it's banned:** Vague audience = vague copy. Specific audience = specific copy.

**Replacement:** One specific buyer. Speak their language.

---

## 20. The "fake scarcity + fake urgency + fake bonus stack" combo

**What it looks like:** "Last chance! Only 3 spots! Total value $11,458! 24 hours left!"

**Why it's banned:** All three of these anti-patterns together read as a scam. Buyers bounce immediately.

**Replacement:** Real scarcity, real urgency (or none), real bonus values.

---

## Quick checklist (use before shipping any copy)

Before copy goes live, every item must be NO:

- [ ] No banned hype words in body copy
- [ ] No fake urgency
- [ ] No corporate sludge
- [ ] No generic testimonials (all have specifics)
- [ ] No fake scarcity
- [ ] No buried CTAs (visible above and within page)
- [ ] No unanchored prices
- [ ] No fake authority (real proof only)
- [ ] No surface/offer mismatch
- [ ] No false scarcity in headlines
- [ ] No headline/body mismatch
- [ ] No entry pop-ups
- [ ] No "click here" CTAs
- [ ] No buried price
- [ ] No fake "not for everyone" framing
- [ ] No hidden fees
- [ ] No bonus inflation
- [ ] No copy that doesn't match the buyer
- [ ] No stacked fakes (scarcity + urgency + bonus)

If any item is YES, fix before shipping. Copy anti-patterns erode trust faster than almost any other mistake.
