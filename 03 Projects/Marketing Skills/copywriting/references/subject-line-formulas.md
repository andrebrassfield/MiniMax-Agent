# Subject Line Formulas — 12 Templates + Testing

The subject line decides whether the email gets opened. Body copy decides whether it gets read. CTA decides whether it converts. Get the subject line wrong and nothing else matters.

These 12 formulas cover most high-performing subject lines. Pick the one that fits the email's job.

---

## The 12 formulas

### 1. Curiosity (open loop)

> "The pricing mistake that cost me $40k"

**Anatomy:** Tease the answer without giving it. Open a loop the email closes.

**When it works:** Educational emails, story-based content, mechanism reveals.

**Risk:** If the email doesn't deliver on the curiosity, you lose trust. "You'll never believe what happened next" with a boring story is worse than no email.

---

### 2. Specific number

> "I added $40k MRR in 90 days. Here's how."

**Anatomy:** Specific outcome + number + timeframe.

**When it works:** Case studies, results announcements, offer-related emails.

**Risk:** Number must be defensible. Inflated numbers lose trust fast.

---

### 3. Direct + personal

> "Sarah, your offer is waiting"

**Anatomy:** First name + direct statement.

**When it works:** Cart-open, abandoned cart, post-purchase. When the email is about the recipient specifically.

**Risk:** Over-uses personalization. Don't use "Sarah" in every email — it loses effect.

---

### 4. Question

> "What if you could add $40k MRR in 90 days?"

**Anatomy:** Question + outcome.

**When it works:** Re-engagement, lead-gen, top-of-funnel. Buyer opens because they want the answer.

**Risk:** Questions can feel like clickbait. Don't use "?" if the email doesn't answer it.

---

### 5. Urgency (real)

> "Doors close at 5pm ET today"

**Anatomy:** Time-bound action.

**When it works:** Cart-close, last-call, deadline-driven emails. ONLY with real urgency.

**Risk:** Manufactured urgency ("24 hours left!" with no deadline) trains buyers to ignore you.

---

### 6. List

> "5 pricing mistakes B2B founders make"

**Anatomy:** Number + problem + audience.

**When it works:** Educational emails, content upgrades, lead magnets.

**Risk:** Number must match. "5 mistakes" with 3 in the email breaks trust.

---

### 7. How-to

> "How to add $40k MRR without paid ads"

**Anatomy:** How-to + outcome + objection-buster.

**When it works:** Educational content, mechanism reveals.

**Risk:** "How to" can feel like content. Works best for educational emails, not sales.

---

### 8. Negation / contrarian

> "Don't raise your prices"

**Anatomy:** Counter-intuitive statement.

**When it works:** Mechanism reveals, contrarian content, mid-launch differentiation.

**Risk:** Contrarian must be defensible. "Don't raise prices" because you have a different view works; "Don't raise prices" because you have no view doesn't.

---

### 9. Announcement

> "The 90-Day MRR Sprint is open"

**Anatomy:** Direct announcement.

**When it works:** Cart-open, product launch, new offering.

**Risk:** Announcement without context lands flat. Pair with "for [audience] who [outcome]."

---

### 10. Reference / proof

> "How Sarah added $40k MRR"

**Anatomy:** Named result + outcome.

**When it works:** Case-study emails, social proof, mid-launch.

**Risk:** Use real names with permission. Made-up testimonials are anti-pattern (see below).

---

### 11. Re-engagement

> "Still interested in [outcome]?"

**Anatomy:** Direct question + outcome.

**When it works:** Re-engagement sequences, lapsed subscribers, abandoned cart follow-up.

**Risk:** Don't use too early. Subscriber hasn't opened in 60+ days before this is appropriate.

---

### 12. Time-bound

> "Today only: 10% off the 90-Day MRR Sprint"

**Anatomy:** Time + offer + incentive.

**When it works:** Launch-week pricing, flash sales, last-day offers.

**Risk:** Time-bound must be real. "Today only" repeated weekly destroys trust.

---

## Picking the right formula

| Email job | Use this formula |
|-----------|------------------|
| Welcome / deliver promise | Direct + personal (3) or Announcement (9) |
| Educational content | Curiosity (1), List (6), How-to (7), or Negation (8) |
| Case study / proof | Specific number (2) or Reference (10) |
| Cart open | Announcement (9) or Direct + personal (3) |
| Mid-launch objection handling | Question (4) or Curiosity (1) |
| Cart close | Urgency (5) or Time-bound (12) |
| Abandoned cart | Direct + personal (3) or Re-engagement (11) |
| Re-engagement | Re-engagement (11) or Question (4) |

## Subject line anti-patterns

### ALL CAPS
"OPEN THIS NOW" reads as desperate and gets flagged as spam.

### Excessive punctuation
"$$$ Save BIG!!!" reads as spam. Filters catch this.

### Spam trigger words
"Free," "guaranteed," "act now," "limited time," "click here." Email filters flag these.

### Misleading
"This one weird trick..." with no weird trick. Subject lines must deliver on the promise.

### Too long
Subject lines over 60 characters get cut off on mobile. Front-load the important words.

### Too short (with no signal)
"Quick question" with no specificity. Doesn't earn the open.

### Emoji overload
"🎉🚀💰 BIG NEWS! 🎉" reads as spam.

### Same subject line twice
Sends the same subject line to a subscriber twice in 30 days. They'll stop opening.

### Fake personalization
"Sarah, this is urgent" sent to a list where most names aren't Sarah. Or worse, fake "Sarah" tags.

### Sender name as subject line
"FWD: FWD: FWD: re: re:" — email chains from years ago. Don't.

## Pre-headers

The pre-header is the second line in inbox — visible after the subject on most clients. It reinforces the subject or adds a second hook.

**Rules:**
- Reinforce the subject, don't repeat it.
- Add a second hook (urgency, specificity, mechanism).
- 40–90 characters.
- Never start with "View this email in your browser" — wastes the slot.

**Examples:**

| Subject | Bad pre-header | Good pre-header |
|---------|----------------|-----------------|
| "Cart opens tomorrow" | "View in browser" | "8 founders per cohort — first come, first served" |
| "The pricing mistake that cost me $40k" | "View in browser" | "I made it for 18 months before I saw the pattern" |
| "Sarah, your offer is waiting" | "View in browser" | "Your seat is reserved for 48 more hours" |

## Subject line testing methodology

### A/B test 2–3 variants

Don't send the email without testing. Write 2–3 subject lines per email. Test the top 2.

### Sample size

At least 200 recipients per arm to draw conclusions. Below that, noise dominates.

### Test ONE variable

Subject line only. Don't test subject + pre-header + sender name simultaneously.

### What to measure

Open rate is the primary metric. Click-through and conversion are secondary — a subject that gets opens but lower clicks is still a good subject if the body needs work.

### Send time

Test subject lines, not send times. Send time is a separate optimization. Don't conflate.

### Don't test everything

If you test every email's subject line, you never learn what works. Test 1 in 3 emails, ship the others on best judgment.

## Sender name

Sender name affects open rate as much as subject line.

**Best practices:**
- Use a person's name (not a brand name). "Sarah from [Brand]" > "[Brand]".
- Be consistent. Don't switch between "Sarah" and "The [Brand] Team."
- Don't include "no-reply@..." — even if technically a no-reply, the sender name should look human.

---

## Subject line optimization loop

After each send:

1. **Check open rate** vs. your benchmark.
2. **Note the formula** that worked.
3. **Hypothesis for next send.**
4. **Test 2 variants** if uncertain.
5. **Build a pattern library** of what works for your audience.

Over 6 months, you'll know your audience's subject line patterns — curiosity vs. specificity vs. direct. Use that data.

---

## Quick checklist

Before shipping any subject line:

- [ ] 30–60 characters (mobile-safe)
- [ ] No ALL CAPS
- [ ] No excessive punctuation
- [ ] No spam trigger words
- [ ] Specific > vague
- [ ] Front-loaded important words
- [ ] Pre-header reinforces (not repeats) subject
- [ ] Sender name is a person
- [ ] Subject delivers on the email body's promise
