# Voice & Tone Rules — The Canonical Voice

The voice is the operator's voice — not a "brand voice." When copy sounds like the operator talking to a smart friend, it converts. When copy sounds like "marketing voice" — corporate, padded, soft — it doesn't.

This document is the canonical voice for the Marketing Skills v2.5.0 set. Adapt it for the operator's specifics; don't replace it with generic "brand voice."

---

## The 7 voice rules

### Rule 1: Specific beats generic — always

**Generic:** "We help businesses grow."
**Specific:** "We help B2B SaaS founders add $40k MRR in 90 days."

**Generic:** "Our approach is unique."
**Specific:** "We use the 4-Block Pricing Method — pricing, outbound, close rate, retention."

**Generic:** "You'll see results."
**Specific:** "You'll have a written pricing audit by Day 14 and an outbound plan by Day 30."

The test: can the buyer visualize what they're buying? If not, get more specific.

---

### Rule 2: Short sentences mixed with medium

**Bad:** "Our methodology, which has been refined over 12 years of working with founders just like you, combines proven frameworks with personalized attention to ensure that your specific situation is addressed in a way that maximizes your outcomes."

**Good:** "Our methodology has 12 years behind it. We combine proven frameworks with personalized attention. Your specific situation gets addressed."

**Default sentence length:** 8–20 words. Mix in occasional 4–6 word punchy sentences. Avoid 30+ word sentences.

---

### Rule 3: Active voice, dominant

**Passive:** "The pricing audit will be delivered by Day 14."
**Active:** "You'll have the pricing audit by Day 14."

**Passive:** "Outreach templates have been created by our team."
**Active:** "Our team creates the outreach templates."

Default to active. Use passive only when the actor genuinely doesn't matter.

---

### Rule 4: No filler

Cut every phrase that doesn't add information.

**Filler to cut:**
- "In today's fast-paced world"
- "Solutions-driven approach"
- "Value-added services"
- "Leverage synergies"
- "At the end of the day"
- "When all is said and done"
- "It's important to note that"
- "I wanted to reach out"
- "Just wanted to touch base"
- "Hope this finds you well"

**Replacement:** State the thing. Don't soften it.

---

### Rule 5: Operator's voice > brand voice

The operator's voice is what makes copy feel human. "Brand voice" without an operator behind it sounds like every other brand.

**Test:** Read the copy out loud. If it sounds like something you'd say to a smart friend over coffee, it's right. If it sounds like a press release, rewrite.

**Operator voice traits** (use as defaults):
- Direct (no hedging).
- Specific (numbers, names, mechanisms).
- Opinionated (willing to say what's wrong).
- Brief (cuts filler).
- Honest (admits limitations).

**Brand voice traits to avoid:**
- Hedged ("may potentially help")
- Generic ("solutions for modern businesses")
- Soft ("we'd love to support you on your journey")
- Corporate ("synergize your value-add")

---

### Rule 6: Write like a builder, not a marketer

Builders talk about what they built and how it works. Marketers talk about positioning and benefits.

**Marketer voice:** "Unlock your potential with our revolutionary platform."

**Builder voice:** "We built this for founders who want to add $40k MRR without hiring a sales team. Here's how it works."

**Builder voice traits:**
- "Here's how it works"
- "We built this because..."
- "The mechanism is..."
- "Here's what we found..."
- "Most people get this wrong because..."

**Marketer voice traits to avoid:**
- "Unlock your potential"
- "Revolutionary platform"
- "Transform your business"
- "Discover the power of..."

---

### Rule 7: Honest about limitations

Strong copy admits what the offer isn't. Weak copy over-promises.

**Weak:** "This is for everyone."

**Strong:** "This is for B2B SaaS founders at $1k–$10k MRR. If you're pre-revenue or above $50k MRR, this isn't for you."

Admitting limitations builds trust. The buyer who matches the offer trusts you more. The buyer who doesn't match self-selects out.

## Tone adjustments by surface

The voice stays the same. The tone shifts by surface.

### Sales page

- More confident (the offer is the answer)
- More direct (push toward action)
- More proof-heavy (specific case studies)
- More urgency (real scarcity, real deadlines)

### Email

- More conversational (1:1, like a letter)
- More personal (first name, specific references)
- Less aggressive (email is relationship, sales page is conversion)
- Shorter paragraphs (4–6 lines max)

### Ad copy

- Punchier (3–5 words headline, 10–15 word body)
- More outcome-forward (the ad's job is to get the click)
- More direct (less setup, more hook)

### Sales calls

- Conversational (the buyer is on the phone)
- More listening (questions, not monologues)
- More story (the operator's experience is the proof)
- Less pitchy (sales calls feel like help, not pitch)

---

## Voice consistency check

Before copy ships, read it aloud. Does it sound like the same person across:
- The headline
- The body
- The CTAs
- The emails
- The sales calls

If the sales page sounds like the operator but the emails sound like a corporation, fix the emails. Voice mismatch breaks trust.

## Voice calibration examples

### Same offer, three voices

**Offer:** "The 90-Day MRR Sprint"

**Marketer voice:**
> "Unlock unprecedented MRR growth with our comprehensive 90-day sprint solution. Our revolutionary methodology combines cutting-edge pricing strategies with proven outbound frameworks to deliver transformational results for ambitious founders."

**Builder voice (Dre-style):**
> "Add $40k MRR in 90 days. Here's how: we rebuild your pricing, your outbound, and your close rate — the three levers that actually move MRR for B2B SaaS at the $1k–$10k stage. We cap each cohort at 8 founders. Next cohort opens Q1 2027."

**Generic "professional" voice:**
> "Our 90-day program is designed to help B2B SaaS founders accelerate revenue growth through proven methodologies in pricing and outbound strategy."

The builder voice wins every time. Specific numbers, named mechanisms, real constraints, no filler.

## Voice anti-patterns

### "Revolutionary" / "game-changing" / "breakthrough"
Banned hype words. See anti-patterns checklist.

### "Solutions-driven approach"
Corporate sludge. Cut.

### "We'd love to support you on your journey"
Soft. Replace with what you actually do.

### "Whether you're a..."
Long conditional sentences that try to include everyone. Cut the people who don't fit.

### "As someone who..."
Operator-as-narrator filler. State the thing.

### "I hope this email finds you well"
Hollow opener. State why you're writing.

### Em-dashes used as filler
"We believe—and this is important—that your success..." Em-dashes aren't a substitute for cutting the sentence.

### Bullet points where prose would be clearer
Lists for the sake of lists. Use prose when prose is clearer.

### Bold/italic as decoration
**This is fine**, but using bold for every other word dilutes emphasis. Use bold for what actually matters.

## Voice for different operators

The voice rules stay the same. The tone adapts to the operator's natural style.

### Operators who are direct and technical
- Voice: terse, mechanism-forward, no fluff
- Example: "Here's the architecture. Here's what we built. Here's the price."

### Operators who are warm and relational
- Voice: more story, more personal, but still specific
- Example: "I built this because I had the same problem three years ago. Here's how I solved it."

### Operators who are provocative and contrarian
- Voice: opinionated, willing to say what's wrong
- Example: "Most pricing advice is wrong. Here's why, and here's what to do instead."

Pick the operator's natural style. Don't impose a voice that doesn't fit.

---

## Voice and the test of "would I say this?"

The strongest voice test:

> "Would I say this sentence out loud to a smart friend at a coffee shop?"

If yes, ship. If no, rewrite.

The test catches:
- Corporate language ("synergize", "value-add")
- Over-formal construction ("It is imperative that...")
- Padding ("In today's fast-paced world...")
- Soft hedging ("We'd love to potentially help...")
- False enthusiasm ("We're SO excited to...")

The operator's spoken voice is the strongest copy voice. Write like the operator talks.

## Quick checklist

Before any copy ships:

- [ ] Specific > generic throughout
- [ ] Sentences average 8–20 words
- [ ] Active voice dominant
- [ ] No filler phrases
- [ ] Sounds like the operator (not "brand voice")
- [ ] Builder voice, not marketer voice
- [ ] Honest about limitations
- [ ] Reads aloud as natural speech
- [ ] Same voice across surfaces
- [ ] No banned hype words
