# CTA Design — Copy, Placement, Psychology

The CTA (call-to-action) is where the offer converts or doesn't. A weak CTA loses the buyer who got all the way through the page. A strong CTA converts the buyer who was ready to act.

CTA design has three parts: copy, placement, and friction reduction.

---

## CTA copy

### First-person vs second-person

**First-person ("I"):** Higher click-through on most surfaces. Buyer takes ownership.

> "Yes, I want to add $40k MRR."
> "Start my 90-day sprint."
> "Get my pricing audit."

**Second-person ("You"):** Slightly weaker but works for instructional framing.

> "Add $40k MRR."
> "Get started today."
> "Reserve your spot."

**Default rule:** first-person for the primary CTA. Second-person for secondary CTAs.

### Button copy anti-patterns

| Don't | Do |
|-------|-----|
| "Submit" | "Start my sprint" |
| "Click here" | "Yes, I want the 90-Day MRR Sprint" |
| "Learn more" | "See the full offer" |
| "Buy now" | "Join the cohort — $7,500" |
| "Sign up" | "Reserve my seat" |
| "Get started" | "Begin the audit" |

The button should describe what the buyer GETS, not what they DO. "I want to start" is the buyer's mental model; "submit" is the operator's mental model.

### Length

**Short CTA (3–5 words):** Works when context is clear. "Start the sprint."

**Medium CTA (5–10 words):** Default. Carries offer + action. "Start the 90-Day MRR Sprint."

**Long CTA (10–20 words):** Works when the offer is expensive or the action is high-commitment. "Yes, I want to add $40k MRR in 90 days — $7,500."

**Anti-pattern:** CTA that's longer than a sentence. Buyers stop reading buttons past 20 words.

### Including the price

For high-ticket offers, include the price in the button. It reduces friction (no surprise at checkout).

> "Start the 90-Day MRR Sprint — $7,500"
> "Reserve my VIP seat — $2,997"

For low-ticket, the price in the button is overkill.

> "Get the template pack"

### Including the urgency

If the offer has real scarcity, the CTA can echo it.

> "Reserve my seat — only 4 left"

If the offer has no scarcity, don't manufacture it in the CTA.

## Placement

### Single primary CTA

One primary CTA per surface. The buyer should never wonder where to click.

**Sales page:** one CTA above the fold (small) + one CTA in the offer section + one CTA at the bottom (large). All link to the same checkout.

**Email:** one CTA per email. One button. One link. No competing CTAs.

**Landing page:** one CTA. Period.

### Above the fold

The first CTA appears above the fold, but it doesn't have to be the primary conversion CTA. Use it to "warm" the buyer — a smaller CTA that says "see the offer" or "learn more."

**Above-the-fold CTA options:**
- "See the offer" — for cold traffic that needs more setup.
- "Reserve my seat" — for warm traffic that's ready.
- The full primary CTA — for repeat visitors / warm lists.

### Mid-page CTA

Mid-page CTA catches buyers who got the message but aren't ready to scroll to the bottom. The mid-page CTA is usually the same as the bottom CTA.

### Final CTA

The bottom CTA is the biggest. Restate the offer, restate the guarantee, push to action.

**Anti-pattern:** multiple final CTAs at the bottom. Split attention. Pick one.

### Mobile

On mobile, the CTA should be:
- Above the fold (the buyer shouldn't have to scroll to act).
- Big enough to tap easily (44×44 px minimum).
- Sticky if the page is long (sticky CTA on mobile lifts conversion 10–30%).

## Friction reduction

The CTA's job is to reduce friction between "I want this" and "I clicked."

### Repeat the offer

Just before the CTA, restate what they get. Don't make the buyer scroll up.

> **Yes, I want to add $40k MRR in 90 days.**
> The 90-Day MRR Sprint. 12 weekly calls. Written pricing audit by Day 14. 90-day conditional guarantee.
>
> [RESERVE MY SEAT — $7,500]

### Repeat the guarantee

Right before or right after the CTA, restate the guarantee.

> ... 90-day conditional money-back guarantee. Complete the work, get the result, or full refund.

### Reduce perceived risk

The CTA section should not introduce new risks. No new objections should surface in the CTA section — handle them earlier.

### Match the page above

If the page promises "12 weekly calls," the CTA should mention "12 weekly calls." Consistency matters.

### Remove optionality when possible

One CTA > "Click to choose your tier." Let the buyer click first, then choose tier on the next page. Don't make the CTA do too much.

**Exception:** when tier choice is the buyer's primary decision (and the tiers are genuinely different), show all three as CTAs.

---

## CTA section anatomy

A complete CTA section has:

1. **Restate outcome** — "Ready to add $40k MRR in 90 days?"
2. **Restate offer** — "The 90-Day MRR Sprint."
3. **Repeat guarantee** — "90-day conditional money-back."
4. **Primary button** — "Yes, I want to add $40k MRR — $7,500"
5. **Secondary option** — Payment structure ("or 3 × $2,750")
6. **Final risk-removal** — "Cancel anytime. No long-term contract."

## CTA copy by surface

### Sales page

**Above the fold (small):** "See the offer →" or "How it works"
**Mid-page:** "Yes, I want to start — $7,500"
**Bottom (primary):** "Reserve my seat — $7,500 (or 3 × $2,750)"

### Email

**Primary:** "Reserve my seat" or "Get the template" — first-person, short.

**Link text in body:** Repeat the offer in the link, not "click here."

### Landing page

**Primary:** Single CTA. Restate the offer. First-person. "Yes, I want the [offer name]."

### Pop-up / slide-in

**Primary:** Short CTA. The buyer is leaving — give them one clear action.

> "Wait — get 10% off when you join today."

(Only if you have a real reason to discount. Don't manufacture exit-intent discounts.)

## CTA anti-patterns

### Generic button copy
"Submit," "Click here," "Learn more." Loses 10–30% of conversion vs. specific copy.

### Multiple competing CTAs
"Buy now / Schedule a call / Download the PDF / Follow us on Twitter." The buyer picks none.

### CTA that doesn't match the page
"Schedule a demo" on a sales page for a self-serve product.

### CTA that introduces new info
"Subscribe to our newsletter" on a sales page. The buyer came to buy, not subscribe.

### CTA buried in body copy
The button is 2,000 words deep. Buyers don't scroll that far.

### Sticky CTA on a short page
Sticky CTAs on pages under 600px tall look desperate.

### CTA without offer context
"Yes!" with no offer context. The buyer has to scroll up to remember what they're saying yes to.

## CTA testing methodology

### Test button copy

Test 2–3 button copy variants. Measure click-through, not conversion.

### Test button color

Test 1–2 colors. Keep the contrast high (button stands out from page background). Don't test 5+ colors — you're reading noise.

### Test CTA placement

Above-fold vs. mid-page vs. bottom — these often perform differently on cold vs. warm traffic.

### Test first-person vs. second-person

A/B test. First-person usually wins, but not always.

### What to measure

- Click-through rate on the CTA (primary)
- Conversion rate past the CTA (secondary)
- Revenue per visitor (the metric that matters)

Don't optimize click-through alone — a CTA that gets more clicks but lower conversion is a worse CTA.

---

## Quick checklist

Before shipping a CTA:

- [ ] First-person ("I want to...") for primary CTA
- [ ] Describes what the buyer gets, not what they do
- [ ] Includes price for high-ticket offers
- [ ] Includes scarcity echo if real scarcity exists
- [ ] Above-the-fold CTA exists (even if smaller)
- [ ] One primary CTA per surface (no competing CTAs)
- [ ] Restates the offer just before the CTA
- [ ] Restates the guarantee just before or after the CTA
- [ ] No new information introduced in the CTA section
- [ ] Mobile CTA is tappable (44×44 px) and above the fold
