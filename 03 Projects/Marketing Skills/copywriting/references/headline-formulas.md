# Headline Formulas — 12 Templates

The headline is the highest-leverage element on any sales surface. A better headline can lift conversion 30–100% without changing anything else. A bad headline kills the page before any other element gets a chance.

These 12 formulas cover most high-performing headlines. Pick the one that fits the offer; don't stack multiple formulas in one headline.

---

## The 12 formulas

### 1. Outcome-shaped

> "Add $40k MRR in 90 days"

**Anatomy:** Specific outcome + number + timeframe.

**When it works:** Offer has a specific, measurable outcome. Default for most offers.

**Risk:** Number must be defensible. "$1M in 30 days" loses trust.

---

### 2. Outcome + audience

> "Founders: add $40k MRR in 90 days"

**Anatomy:** Audience + outcome.

**When it works:** Audience is specific. The buyer should see themselves in the headline.

**Risk:** Audience must be small enough that "founders" doesn't read as vague.

---

### 3. Outcome + mechanism

> "Add $40k MRR in 90 days using the 4-Block Pricing Method"

**Anatomy:** Outcome + named mechanism.

**When it works:** Mechanism is differentiated and explainable. Buyer needs to understand HOW.

**Risk:** Mechanism name must be specific. "The Method" doesn't differentiate.

---

### 4. Outcome + objection-buster

> "Add $40k MRR in 90 days — without hiring a sales team"

**Anatomy:** Outcome + objection handled.

**When it works:** The buyer has a known objection that's blocking conversion. Objection-buster removes it.

**Risk:** Don't use unless the objection is real. Made-up objections read as filler.

---

### 5. Outcome + speed

> "Land your first 10 customers in 30 days"

**Anatomy:** Outcome + tight timeframe.

**When it works:** Speed is the differentiator vs alternatives. "Most coaches take 12 months, we do 30 days."

**Risk:** Speed claim must be defensible. "10 customers in 30 days" with no mechanism fails.

---

### 6. Numbered list

> "7 pricing mistakes that are killing your MRR"

**Anatomy:** Number + problem + outcome implied.

**When it works:** Listicle-style offers, content upgrades, lead magnets. Also works on sales pages as a hook.

**Risk:** Number must match the actual content. "7 mistakes" with 5 in the copy breaks trust.

---

### 7. Question

> "What if you could add $40k MRR in 90 days?"

**Anatomy:** Question + outcome.

**When it works:** Buyer is open to being led. Question opens a loop the page fills.

**Risk:** Questions can feel manipulative. Use sparingly. Better for cold traffic.

---

### 8. How-to

> "How to add $40k MRR in 90 days without paid ads"

**Anatomy:** How-to + outcome + objection-buster.

**When it works:** Educational offers. Buyer wants the method, not just the outcome.

**Risk:** "How to" can feel like content, not offer. Best for lead-gen pages.

---

### 9. Negative option

> "Stop guessing about pricing. Start adding $40k MRR."

**Anatomy:** Stop X + Start Y.

**When it works:** Buyer is in a stuck state. The negative frames what they're escaping.

**Risk:** Negative framing can feel heavy. Pair with a positive.

---

### 10. Direct address

> "Founder: if you're stuck at $5k MRR, this is for you."

**Anatomy:** Direct audience address + specific state + offer.

**When it works:** Audience has a specific situation. The headline identifies them.

**Risk:** "If you're stuck at $5k MRR" is specific but loses buyers who aren't stuck at exactly $5k. Use ranges or broader states.

---

### 11. Before-after-bridge

> "From $4k MRR to $40k MRR in 90 days — here's how"

**Anatomy:** Before state + after state + timeframe.

**When it works:** Transformation is the offer. Reader sees themselves in the "before" and wants the "after."

**Risk:** Before state must be aspirational-but-believable. "$0 to $1M" is unbelievable.

---

### 12. Mechanism-named

> "The 4-Block Pricing Method: How B2B SaaS Founders Add $40k MRR"

**Anatomy:** Method name + audience + outcome.

**When it works:** Method IS the offer's differentiation. Buyer recognizes the method as a brand.

**Risk:** Method names must be earnable. "The Smith Method" with no track record fails.

---

## Picking the right formula

| If your offer has... | Use this formula |
|----------------------|------------------|
| Specific measurable outcome | Outcome-shaped (1) |
| Specific audience | Outcome + audience (2) |
| Differentiated mechanism | Outcome + mechanism (3) |
| Known buyer objection | Outcome + objection-buster (4) |
| Speed as differentiator | Outcome + speed (5) |
| Content / lead magnet | Numbered list (6) or How-to (8) |
| Buyer is stuck | Negative option (9) or Direct address (10) |
| Transformation focus | Before-after-bridge (11) |
| Brand-name method | Mechanism-named (12) |

## Headline anti-patterns

### Vague outcome
"Transform your business." "Reach your goals." "Unlock your potential." No specifics, no measurement, no audience.

### Clever for clever's sake
"The pricing whisperer." "Revenue alchemist." Clever doesn't sell. Specific sells.

### Banned hype words
"Revolutionary," "game-changing," "breakthrough," "magic." See `/offers` anti-patterns.

### Two headlines in one
"Add $40k MRR in 90 days OR learn the secrets of high-growth founders." Pick one. Two headlines split attention.

### Long headline (over 15 words)
Headlines lose power past 15 words. Sub-headline carries the rest.

### Headline without a number
Numbers anchor attention. Headlines with numbers outperform headlines without.

### Headline that buries the outcome
"The story of how we figured out pricing after 50 failed attempts." Cute. Loses buyers who don't want the story.

## Headline testing methodology

### Test 2–3 variants per offer

Write 2–3 headlines from different formulas. A/B test the page with each.

**Sample size:** 200+ visitors per arm before drawing conclusions. Below that, you're reading noise.

### Test the headline, not the whole page

If you change the headline + sub-headline + body, you don't know which moved the result. Change the headline only.

### Test against current, not just against each other

Run the current headline against the new one. Sometimes the current is the winner even after "improving."

### What to measure

Click-through from headline to next section. For sales pages: scroll depth past the headline. For emails: open rate.

Headline tests that look at conversion (sales) need much larger samples — 1,000+ visitors per arm.

## Worked example: same offer, 3 headlines

**Offer:** "The 90-Day MRR Sprint" — coaching for B2B SaaS founders.

| # | Formula | Headline |
|---|---------|----------|
| 1 | Outcome-shaped | "Add $40k MRR in 90 days." |
| 2 | Outcome + mechanism | "Add $40k MRR in 90 days using the 4-Block Pricing Method." |
| 3 | Before-after-bridge | "From $4k MRR to $40k MRR in 90 days — without hiring a sales team." |

**Test hypothesis:** Headline 3 will convert best because the before-state is specific and aspirational-believable, and the objection-buster ("without hiring") removes the most common blocker.

If 3 wins, the page below should reinforce the same before-state specificity throughout.

## Headline sub-headline pair

The headline opens the loop. The sub-headline closes it just enough to keep reading.

**Pairing rules:**
- Sub-headline reinforces, doesn't repeat.
- Sub-headline adds proof, mechanism, or differentiator.
- Sub-headline doesn't introduce new questions.

**Example pair:**

> # Add $40k MRR in 90 days
> The 12-week coaching engagement built around the 4-Block Pricing Method — used by 47 B2B SaaS founders to add $30k–$80k MRR.

The sub-headline adds mechanism (4-Block Pricing Method) and proof (47 founders, specific range).

## Quick checklist

Before shipping a headline:

- [ ] Specific outcome (no generic "grow" / "transform")
- [ ] Number or timeframe (anchors attention)
- [ ] 8–15 words
- [ ] No banned hype words
- [ ] Buyer can tell a friend what they get from the headline alone
- [ ] No two-headlines-in-one
- [ ] No clever-for-clever's-sake
- [ ] Sub-headline reinforces without repeating
