# Sales Page Structure — The 8 Sections

A long-form sales page is built from 8 sections, in order. Get the order wrong and the buyer doesn't get to the offer. Get the order right and the offer feels inevitable.

This is the canonical structure. Most high-converting long-form sales pages follow this skeleton. Specific tactics vary by industry and offer.

---

## The 8 sections, in order

### 1. Headline

**Job:** Communicate the outcome in 8–12 words. The buyer should know what they're getting before they read another word.

**Word count:** 8–12 words.

**Anatomy:** Outcome + specificity + (optional: mechanism or audience).

**Examples:**

| Pattern | Example |
|---------|---------|
| Outcome-shaped | "Add $40k MRR in 90 days" |
| Outcome + audience | "Founders: add $40k MRR in 90 days" |
| Outcome + mechanism | "Add $40k MRR in 90 days using the 4-Block Pricing Method" |
| Outcome + time + objection | "Add $40k MRR in 90 days — without hiring a sales team" |

**Anti-patterns:**
- Vague ("Transform your business")
- Clever-for-clever's-sake ("Unlock your potential")
- Hype-laden ("The revolutionary new way to...")

**Test:** Can the buyer tell a friend what the offer does based on the headline alone?

---

### 2. Sub-headline

**Job:** Add mechanism, differentiator, or social proof. Reinforces the headline without repeating it.

**Word count:** 12–25 words.

**Anatomy:** Mechanism + (optional: proof or differentiator).

**Examples:**

> "Add $40k MRR in 90 days using the 4-Block Pricing Method — the same system that took [client] from $0 to $50k MRR in 11 weeks."

**Sub-headline does one of:**
- Names the mechanism ("the 4-Block Pricing Method")
- Adds proof ("used by 200+ founders")
- Differentiates from alternatives ("without paid ads / without hiring / in 90 days not 12 months")
- Combines 2–3 of the above

---

### 3. Problem

**Job:** Articulate the buyer's pain in their language. The buyer should feel seen.

**Word count:** 150–300 words.

**Anatomy:** Specific pain + frequency + cost.

**Example:**

> "You've tried the launches. The cold emails. The 'wait for product-market fit' advice. Six months in, your MRR is stuck at $4k, your runway is shrinking, and you don't know which lever to pull first. Every week you wait, you're burning $5–10k in potential revenue. The advice is generic, the courses are vague, and you can't afford to waste another quarter guessing."

**What makes a good Problem section:**
- Speaks the buyer's language (their words, not yours).
- Names the specific cost (revenue lost, time wasted, opportunity missed).
- Sets up the Agitation that follows.

**Anti-patterns:**
- Generic pain ("Are you struggling with marketing?")
- Operator-centric ("I noticed that many founders...")
- Too short — doesn't earn the right to the Solution.

---

### 4. Agitation

**Job:** Make the cost of inaction vivid. Why does staying stuck cost more than buying?

**Word count:** 100–250 words.

**Anatomy:** Future cost + irreversibility + emotional stakes.

**Example:**

> "Here's what staying stuck actually costs: at your current $4k MRR and a $30k/mo burn rate, you have maybe 6 months of runway left. After that, you're either raising at a worse valuation or shutting down. Every month you delay is a month of compounding missed revenue — $30k, $60k, $90k by Q4. The founders who break through aren't smarter. They just have a specific mechanism that fits their situation. That's what this is."

**What makes a good Agitation:**
- Quantifies the cost (specific numbers, not vague).
- Time-binds it (creates urgency without artificial scarcity).
- Sets up the Solution as the obvious next step.

**Anti-patterns:**
- Fear-mongering without specifics ("You'll fail!")
- Manipulative ("Your competitors are already ahead...")
- Too long — burns the buyer's attention.

---

### 5. Solution

**Job:** Introduce the offer as the mechanism that fixes the problem.

**Word count:** 200–400 words.

**Anatomy:** Mechanism introduction + how it works + what makes it different.

**Example:**

> "The 90-Day MRR Sprint is a 12-week coaching engagement built around the 4-Block Pricing Method. We rebuild your pricing, your outbound motion, and your sales-call close rate — the three levers that actually move MRR for B2B SaaS at the $1k–$10k MRR stage.
>
> You don't get generic advice. You get a written pricing audit by Day 14, a 30-day outbound plan by Day 30, and a 12-week playbook you own forever.
>
> This isn't for everyone. We cap each cohort at 8 founders. The next cohort opens Q1 2027."

**What makes a good Solution:**
- Names the mechanism specifically.
- Tells the buyer what they get (deliverables).
- Differentiates from alternatives.
- States scarcity or selection criteria.

**Anti-patterns:**
- "Our program is comprehensive..." (no specifics).
- "We help you with..." (the buyer doesn't care what you help with).
- Too long — burns attention before the offer.

---

### 6. Proof

**Job:** Show that others like the buyer got the result. Move Perceived Likelihood.

**Word count:** 200–600 words (varies widely).

**Anatomy:** Testimonials + case studies + (optional: logos, stats, press).

**Testimonial anatomy:**
- Name + role + company (specific, not generic)
- Before state
- What they did
- Result (specific number)
- Optional quote

**Example:**

> "I was at $3k MRR and burning out on cold email. Three months into the Sprint, we're at $22k MRR. The pricing audit alone was worth 10x the engagement fee."
> — Sarah K., Founder, Lumen Analytics

**Case study anatomy:**
- Buyer profile (specific)
- Problem
- Intervention
- Result (specific)
- Timeframe

**Anti-patterns:**
- Generic testimonials ("This changed my life!")
- Stock-photo headshots with fake quotes.
- "As seen in..." without logo rights.
- Outdated or irrelevant testimonials (B2C testimonial on B2B offer).

---

### 7. Offer + Guarantee

**Job:** State the six components from `/offers`. Make the offer concrete and the risk removed.

**Word count:** 300–600 words.

**Anatomy:** Each of the six components + the guarantee + price with anchor.

**Example structure:**

> ### Here's what you get
>
> 1. **12 weekly calls** with [operator] — 60 min each, $X value
> 2. **Written pricing audit** by Day 14 — $X value
> 3. **Outbound swipe file** (50 templates) — $X value
> 4. **Group office hours** bi-weekly — $X value
> 5. **30-day execution plan** by Week 12 — $X value
> 6. **Lifetime alumni community access** — $X value
>
> **Total value: $X**
> **Your price: $7,500** (or 3 × $2,750, save $750 with full pay)
>
> Most founders pay $25,000+ for a sales consultant who takes 6 months to ramp. We do this in 90 days for $7,500.

**Guarantee placement:** Right after the offer, before the CTA.

**Example:**

> ### Our guarantee
>
> Complete all 12 calls and the homework. If you haven't added at least $15k MRR by Day 90, full refund. No questions asked.

**Anti-patterns:**
- Buried price (find-the-price game).
- Inflated total value (anti-pattern from `/pricing`).
- Generic guarantee ("30-day money-back").
- Bonus list as theater (see `/offers` anti-patterns).

---

### 8. CTA

**Job:** Push the buyer to action. The page has earned the CTA — repeat the offer, lower the friction, ask for the click.

**Word count:** 50–150 words.

**Anatomy:** Restate outcome + restate offer + repeat guarantee + button.

**Example:**

> ### Ready to add $40k MRR in 90 days?
>
> Join the 90-Day MRR Sprint. 8 founders per cohort. Next cohort opens Q1 2027.
>
> [YES, I WANT THE SPRINT — $7,500]  [or 3 × $2,750]
>
> 90-day conditional money-back guarantee. Complete the work, get the result, or your money back.

**CTA anti-patterns:**
- "Submit" / "Click here" / "Learn more" — generic, no specificity.
- Single CTA in a sea of body copy — buried.
- Multiple competing CTAs — split attention.
- CTA without restating the offer — buyer has to scroll up to remember.

---

## Word-count targets

Total long-form sales page: 1,500–3,500 words. Going much over loses readers. Going much under loses conversion.

| Section | Word count |
|---------|-----------|
| Headline | 8–12 words |
| Sub-headline | 12–25 words |
| Problem | 150–300 |
| Agitation | 100–250 |
| Solution | 200–400 |
| Proof | 200–600 |
| Offer + Guarantee | 300–600 |
| CTA | 50–150 |
| **Total** | **~1,500–3,500** |

---

## When to deviate from the structure

| Situation | Deviation |
|-----------|-----------|
| Low-ticket (<$200) | Cut Agitation. Add price early. |
| Cold traffic (no warm audience) | Extend Problem + Agitation. Buyers need more setup. |
| Warm traffic (knows the operator) | Cut to Solution → Proof → Offer. |
| High-ticket B2B | Add FAQ section between Proof and Offer. |
| Repeat-buyer | Cut everything to Offer + CTA. |

The 8-section structure is the default. Deviations only when the situation demands.

---

## Section-by-section checklist

Before the page ships, every section passes its checklist:

- [ ] **Headline:** outcome-shaped, specific, 8–12 words
- [ ] **Sub-headline:** mechanism or differentiator, not a repeat
- [ ] **Problem:** buyer's language, specific cost
- [ ] **Agitation:** quantified cost of inaction, time-bound
- [ ] **Solution:** named mechanism, named deliverables, differentiation
- [ ] **Proof:** specific testimonials with numbers, matching buyer profile
- [ ] **Offer:** all six components stated, anchor + price + payment structure
- [ ] **Guarantee:** specific, conditional or unconditional, terms clear
- [ ] **CTA:** restates offer, button copy specific, repeated guarantee
