# Scarcity Frameworks — 5 Real Patterns

Scarcity is the answer to "why now?" Without it, the buyer parks the decision and the offer leaks revenue forever.

The discipline: scarcity must be **real**, **specific**, **buyer-relevant**, and **aligned**.

---

## Real vs manufactured

| Real scarcity | Manufactured scarcity |
|---------------|----------------------|
| "Cohort closes Friday at 5pm. Next cohort is Q1 2027." | "Only 3 spots left!" (no actual cap) |
| "First 50 buyers get the template pack; rest don't." | "Limited spots available" (no specifics) |
| "Pricing increases to $5,000 on Monday." | "Offer expires in 3:47:12" (evergreen timer) |
| "Bundled with [partner] until [date]." | "Last chance!" (sent weekly) |

Buyers can tell. Real scarcity has a constraint behind it. Manufactured scarcity is theater, and theater erodes trust over time.

---

## The 5 frameworks

### 1. Cohort Cap

**What:** A defined number of buyers per cohort or cohort window.

**Example:** "We cap each cohort at 12 founders. The current cohort has 7 seats; 5 remain. When they're gone, the next cohort opens in 14 weeks."

**When it works:** Cohort-based courses, group coaching, masterminds. Anywhere there's a real per-cohort capacity limit.

**Risk profile:** None if you actually cap. Watch for "soft caps" where the operator keeps adding beyond stated limit — destroys future cohort cap credibility.

**Buyer signal:** Strong. Real constraint, real timeline.

**Anti-pattern to avoid:** State a cap and ignore it. Once buyers find out, the cap loses all force.

---

### 2. Calendar Window

**What:** Open enrollment during specific windows. Closed between windows.

**Example:** "Q1 2027 enrollment is open until January 31. Next window opens July 2027."

**When it works:** Programs with natural rhythm (quarterly cohorts, seasonal businesses, founder-mindset programs that pair with calendar planning).

**Risk profile:** None. The calendar is the constraint.

**Buyer signal:** Moderate to strong. Buyers who plan around the calendar respond well. Spontaneous buyers may bounce.

**Anti-pattern to avoid:** Opening "just one more spot" between windows. Each exception trains the buyer that the window isn't real.

---

### 3. Evergreen Trigger

**What:** The program starts the first Monday after you join — no fixed cohort.

**Example:** "Join any time. Your onboarding starts the first Monday after you sign up."

**When it works:** Self-paced programs, low-ticket offers, scalable services where cohort dynamics don't matter.

**Risk profile:** None — there's no deadline pressure, so there's no false-promise risk. But there's also no urgency.

**Buyer signal:** Weakest of the five. Works for low-friction offers. Doesn't work for high-ticket where the buyer needs a reason to commit now.

**Anti-pattern to avoid:** Pairing an evergreen trigger with a fake countdown. Pick one or the other.

---

### 4. Asset-Limited

**What:** A specific asset (template, recording, partner product) is only available for a limited number of buyers or limited time.

**Example:** "The first 50 buyers get the customer-acquisition template pack (worth $497). After 50 buyers, the offer no longer includes it."

**When it works:** Bonus-stack-driven offers where one bonus is genuinely limited. Creates urgency around a specific asset, not the core offer.

**Risk profile:** Low. You're not capping the offer — you're capping a bonus.

**Buyer signal:** Strong when the asset is genuinely valuable. Weak if the asset is throwaway.

**Anti-pattern to avoid:** Inflating the asset's value. "First 50 buyers get the $2,997 template" — buyers will check.

---

### 5. Partner-Limited

**What:** The offer is bundled with a partner product/service for a limited window.

**Example:** "Until March 15, every buyer also gets a 30-min call with [named partner] — a $1,500 value. After March 15, the offer no longer includes this."

**When it works:** Joint ventures, partner promotions, time-bound collaborations.

**Risk profile:** Low. The partner's calendar is the constraint.

**Buyer signal:** Strong. The partner adds credibility and the date adds urgency.

**Anti-pattern to avoid:** Partner promos that extend forever. Each extension makes the next one less credible.

---

## Picking the right framework

| If your offer is... | Use |
|---------------------|-----|
| Cohort-based course | Cohort cap (1) |
| Quarterly mastermind | Calendar window (2) |
| Self-paced course | Asset-limited (4) to add urgency |
| Low-ticket info product | Evergreen trigger (3) — accept the lower urgency |
| Service / agency | Calendar window (2) or asset-limited (4) |
| High-ticket B2B | Cohort cap (1) if exclusive, or no scarcity (rely on sales process) |
| Joint venture / partner promo | Partner-limited (5) |

---

## Layering scarcity

You can layer frameworks, but each layer must be real.

**Acceptable layering:**
- Cohort cap (12 founders) + calendar window (closes Friday).

**Risky layering:**
- Cohort cap + calendar window + asset-limited + partner-limited. Too many constraints compete for attention.

**Default rule:** one primary scarcity framework. A secondary framework only if it's genuinely true and reinforces the primary.

---

## What kills scarcity

- **Extending a deadline publicly.** Once is fine. Twice starts eroding trust. Three times and buyers will wait every time.
- **Soft caps.** "Only 3 spots!" but you keep adding. The next cohort's cap is theater.
- **Reopening after closure.** "Just one more spot, then we're really closing." Each reopen trains buyers that closure isn't real.
- **Fake timers in evergreen offers.** "5:47:32 remaining" with no actual countdown behind it. Buyers can do the math.
- **Scarcity without substance.** Pressure tactics without a real offer behind them convert once and don't retain.

The strongest scarcity is the one you never extend. The first time you extend a deadline, you've spent trust you won't get back.

---

## What scarcity actually does

Scarcity does three things, in order of importance:

1. **Converts fence-sitters.** The buyer who was going to think about it gets a reason to commit.
2. **Filters out bad-fit buyers.** Real scarcity means the wrong buyers self-select out, which improves downstream satisfaction.
3. **Signals confidence.** "We can afford to limit this" is a stronger message than "please buy."

Scarcity is a Perceived Likelihood lever on the Value Equation. Use it as substance, not theater.
