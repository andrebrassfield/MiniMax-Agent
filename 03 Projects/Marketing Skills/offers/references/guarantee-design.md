# Guarantee Design — 8 Types

A guarantee removes risk. The right guarantee removes MORE risk than the buyer is taking — without exposing the operator to unmanageable downside. Pick the type that fits the offer, the buyer, and the operator's risk tolerance.

---

## The 8 types

### 1. Unconditional Money-Back

**What:** Full refund, any reason, any time within window.

**Example:** "30-day money-back guarantee. No questions asked."

**When it works:** Low-ticket offers ($50–$500), info products, courses with low fulfillment cost. Doesn't work at high-ticket unless the operator has very high product confidence.

**Risk profile:** Low refund rates when the offer is strong (typically <5%). Watch for refund-as-service abuse at higher ticket.

**Buyer signal:** Removes all purchase friction. Converts well at low ticket. Less differentiating at high ticket.

---

### 2. Conditional Money-Back

**What:** Refund if specific conditions are met. The buyer has to do the work.

**Example:** "Complete all four coaching calls, submit the homework, and if you haven't added $15k MRR in 90 days, full refund."

**When it works:** High-ticket offers ($2k+) where the buyer has to engage for the result. Aligns risk to effort.

**Risk profile:** Low — most buyers who do the work get results and don't refund. Conditions must be verifiable.

**Buyer signal:** Strong. Says "we believe in this enough to bet on it."

---

### 3. Performance Guarantee

**What:** Refund (or partial) if a specific metric isn't hit.

**Example:** "We'll get you 100 qualified leads in 60 days or you don't pay."

**When it works:** Lead generation, performance-marketing, agencies. Anywhere the operator has direct control over a measurable output.

**Risk profile:** Medium — the operator's downside scales with their inability to deliver. Use only where the metric is genuinely in their control.

**Buyer signal:** Very strong. Hard to differentiate on if true.

**Caution:** Don't promise metrics outside your control (e.g., "we'll get you 10 customers"). Use metrics inside your control ("we'll deliver 100 leads").

---

### 4. Time-Based Guarantee

**What:** Refund if the result isn't visible by a specific date.

**Example:** "If you don't see your first $1,000 in revenue by Day 30, full refund."

**When it works:** Programs with predictable timelines. Aligns to the buyer's "when will I see this" question.

**Risk profile:** Low — the time window is bounded. Conditions must be met by buyer (e.g., "complete the program").

**Buyer signal:** Strong. Specific and verifiable.

---

### 5. Milestone Guarantee

**What:** Refund at a specific milestone if outcomes are off-track.

**Example:** "At the 30-day mark, if you're not on track to hit the 90-day outcome, you can leave with a full refund."

**When it works:** Longer programs (3+ months) where early momentum predicts late success. Gives the buyer an off-ramp.

**Risk profile:** Low — catches disengagement early. Operator can verify "on track" with reasonable judgment.

**Buyer signal:** Strong. De-risks long commitments.

---

### 6. Partial Guarantee

**What:** Partial refund if conditions partially met.

**Example:** "Complete 50% of the work and don't see results — 50% refund."

**When it works:** Where the operator wants to share risk without taking all of it. Less common but useful in mid-ticket ranges.

**Risk profile:** Low — the operator only refunds if they didn't deliver.

**Buyer signal:** Moderate. Less differentiating than full conditional guarantees.

---

### 7. Rolling-Cohort Guarantee

**What:** "If you're not satisfied, join the next cohort free."

**Example:** "Not getting what you need by Week 4? Drop into the next cohort at no charge."

**When it works:** Cohort-based courses and group programs. Operator retains the customer; buyer gets continuity.

**Risk profile:** Very low — operator trades one cohort slot for another. Strong for retention metrics.

**Buyer signal:** Strong. Says "we want you to succeed eventually, even if not in this cohort."

---

### 8. Anti-Guarantee

**What:** Explicitly state there's no guarantee — and frame why.

**Example:** "We don't offer refunds because we're selective about who we work with and we expect you to be selective about joining. If you're not sure, this isn't for you."

**When it works:** Very high-ticket B2B consulting, exclusive masterminds, or offers where the operator's positioning depends on selectivity.

**Risk profile:** None — no refunds possible.

**Buyer signal:** Mixed. Strong for the right buyer (filters out tire-kickers). Turns off risk-averse buyers.

**Caution:** Only use when the offer genuinely has selection criteria and the operator can defend the no-refund position.

---

## Picking the right type

Use this decision tree:

```
Is the offer under $500?
├── Yes → Unconditional money-back (low cost, high conversion lift)
└── No → Is the outcome in your control?
    ├── Yes → Performance guarantee (with a metric inside your control)
    └── No → Does the buyer have to do work?
        ├── Yes → Conditional money-back or Time-based
        └── No → Is it a long commitment (>3 months)?
            ├── Yes → Milestone guarantee
            └── No → Unconditional money-back

Cohort-based program?
└── Yes → Rolling-cohort guarantee

Very high-ticket ($25k+) with strict selection?
└── Yes → Anti-guarantee (only if positioning supports it)
```

---

## What guarantees actually convert

Three factors matter more than the type:

1. **Specificity.** "30-day money-back guarantee" converts less than "complete the work and don't get the result — full refund in 30 days."
2. **Surface visibility.** Guarantee on the offer surface, not buried in terms.
3. **Operator credibility.** A guarantee is a promise the operator will pay out. Buyers sense which operators will and which won't.

The guarantee is also a Perceived Likelihood lever. A clear guarantee moves the score on the Value Equation's PL axis directly.

---

## What breaks guarantees

- Hidden conditions. "Refund available" → 14 pages of T&Cs. The guarantee stops converting.
- Terms that don't match the offer. 30-day refund on a 12-month program.
- Refunds the operator fights. Every disputed refund that becomes public knowledge kills future guarantee credibility.
- Generic boilerplate. "Satisfaction guaranteed" with no specifics.

If you're going to offer a guarantee, mean it. The offer's strength depends on it.
