# Formats by Business Type

The same six components land differently depending on business type. Pick the format that matches the operator's actual business — not the format that sounds impressive.

---

## Services (consulting, freelance)

### Format patterns

| Pattern | Structure | When it works |
|---------|-----------|---------------|
| **Diagnostic → paid engagement** | Free or low-cost diagnostic (30–60 min) → scoped paid engagement (2–8 weeks) | Buyers don't know what they need until you've diagnosed. Common in consulting. |
| **Project-based** | Fixed scope, fixed price, fixed timeline | Buyers know what they want. Common in freelance and specialized services. |
| **Retainer** | Monthly recurring for ongoing work | Buyers need ongoing support (fractional CMO, fractional CFO). |
| **Advisory** | Monthly or quarterly flat fee for access + a defined number of calls | Senior advisory, board-level work. |

### Component adaptations

- **Core deliverable:** Named project phases or monthly deliverable cadence. Not "support" — concrete artifacts.
- **Bonus stack:** Often lighter or absent. Services rely on the core deliverable's specificity.
- **Guarantee:** Milestone or conditional works well. Performance guarantees are dangerous unless the metric is in your control.
- **Scarcity:** Calendar window or capacity-based ("I take 4 clients per quarter").
- **Name:** Often named after the buyer or their goal. "Founder's Q1 Strategy Sprint" beats "Strategy Consulting".
- **Price:** Project-based ($5k–$50k), retainer ($3k–$20k/month), advisory ($5k–$30k/month).

### Common leak: vague deliverables
"Strategic consulting" with no named deliverables. Buyer can't picture what they're paying for.

## Agencies

### Format patterns

| Pattern | Structure | When it works |
|---------|-----------|---------------|
| **Retainer** | Monthly fee for defined scope of services | Ongoing marketing, design, dev work. |
| **Performance** | Base fee + % of revenue or per-result | Lead gen, paid acquisition. Buyer wants alignment. |
| **Hybrid** | Lower retainer + performance bonus | Common — shares risk both ways. |
| **Project** | One-time, fixed scope, fixed price | Brand launches, website builds, campaign builds. |

### Component adaptations

- **Core deliverable:** Named monthly deliverables + reporting cadence.
- **Bonus stack:** Audit or assessment at the start. Templates the buyer can use internally.
- **Guarantee:** Performance guarantee works ONLY if metric is in your control. Don't promise "10 new customers" — promise "100 qualified leads" or "X pipeline added".
- **Scarcity:** Capacity-based. Real agencies cap client count.
- **Name:** Often named after the result. "Predictable Pipeline Agency" beats "Growth Marketing Services".
- **Price:** Retainer ($3k–$50k/month), project ($10k–$250k), performance (% of revenue or per-result).

### Common leak: deliverables the buyer can't verify
"We're growing your brand" — buyer can't measure brand growth monthly. Deliverables should be measurable: "X pieces of content, X campaigns launched, X metrics reported."

## Courses

### Format patterns

| Pattern | Structure | When it works |
|---------|-----------|---------------|
| **Cohort-based** | Live cohort with weekly calls, 4–12 weeks | Buyers want accountability + community. Higher completion, higher price. |
| **Self-paced** | Pre-recorded, lifetime access | Buyers want flexibility. Lower price, lower completion. |
| **Premium / hybrid** | Self-paced core + live cohort calls | Best of both. Mid-to-high price. |
| **Certification** | Course + certification at end | B2B buyers, professionals. |

### Component adaptations

- **Core deliverable:** Modules + calls + community. Be specific on module count, call frequency, duration.
- **Bonus stack:** Templates, swipe files, community access, office hours. Each must do one of the four jobs.
- **Guarantee:** Cohort-based → rolling-cohort or conditional. Self-paced → unconditional money-back (low fulfillment cost).
- **Scarcity:** Cohort cap (real), calendar window, or asset-limited bonus.
- **Name:** Time-bound outcome. "The 90-Day MRR Sprint" beats "Marketing Course".
- **Price:** Self-paced ($200–$2k), cohort ($1k–$5k), premium ($2k–$10k), certification ($500–$3k).

### Common leak: curriculum-shaped offer
Listing 47 modules. Buyers don't care about modules — they care about the result. Lead with outcome, name the modules second.

## Coaching

### Format patterns

| Pattern | Structure | When it works |
|---------|-----------|---------------|
| **1:1** | Weekly or bi-weekly calls + async support | High-ticket ($5k+). Strong accountability. |
| **Group** | Small group (5–15), weekly calls + community | Mid-ticket ($1k–$5k). Community + accountability. |
| **Mastermind** | Curated small group (8–20), monthly + quarterly | Very high-ticket ($10k–$50k/year). Peer learning. |
| **Hybrid** | 1:1 calls + group component | Mid-to-high ticket. |

### Component adaptations

- **Core deliverable:** Call cadence + async support + named artifacts (plan, audit, etc.).
- **Bonus stack:** Templates, swipe files, group access, partner products. Often lighter than courses.
- **Guarantee:** Conditional is strongest ("do the work, get the result, or refund"). Anti-guarantee works for $25k+ masterminds.
- **Scarcity:** Cohort cap for group/mastermind. Capacity for 1:1.
- **Name:** Outcome-shaped. "Founder's Pricing Sprint" beats "1:1 Coaching".
- **Price:** 1:1 ($5k–$50k/engagement), group ($1k–$10k), mastermind ($10k–$100k/year).

### Common leak: coaching with no artifact
"8 calls over 3 months" with no plan, no template, no named deliverable. Calls are valuable but the offer reads as amorphous. Name the artifact that comes out of the engagement.

## Info Products

### Format patterns

| Pattern | Structure | When it works |
|---------|-----------|---------------|
| **Low-ticket** | $50–$500, often self-paced or short cohort | Volume play. Wide audience. |
| **Mid-ticket** | $500–$2k, often cohort or premium | Balance of accessibility and value. |
| **High-ticket info** | $2k+, often cohort or premium + calls | Positioned as flagship. Lower volume. |
| **Bundle / library** | Multiple products at a bundle price | Retention play. Often paired with subscription. |

### Component adaptations

- **Core deliverable:** Modules, templates, swipe files. Be specific on count.
- **Bonus stack:** The whole offer often IS a stack. Be ruthless about what earns its place.
- **Guarantee:** Unconditional money-back is standard at low-ticket. Conditional or rolling-cohort at mid-to-high.
- **Scarcity:** Asset-limited (first N buyers get bonus) or cohort cap (mid-to-high).
- **Name:** Outcome-shaped. Often paired with a mechanism name.
- **Price:** Low ($50–$500), mid ($500–$2k), high ($2k+).

### Common leak: inflation theater
The most common leak in info products. Stack 12 bonuses with inflated values to manufacture "total value: $5,000, yours for $497." Buyers can do the math. Inflation erodes trust.

## High-Ticket B2B

### Format patterns

| Pattern | Structure | When it works |
|---------|-----------|---------------|
| **Enterprise / annual contract** | $50k–$1M+, multi-year | Large orgs with structured procurement. |
| **POC / pilot** | 30–90 day paid pilot with success criteria | Buyer wants to de-risk before committing. |
| **Implementation project** | Fixed scope, fixed price, fixed timeline | Buyer knows what they want. |
| **Retainer / managed service** | Monthly recurring for ongoing service | Buyer needs ongoing support. |

### Component adaptations

- **Core deliverable:** Named phases with deliverables, milestones, success criteria.
- **Bonus stack:** Often absent or minimal. B2B offers are heavy on the core deliverable.
- **Guarantee:** SLA-based. Performance guarantees tied to specific metrics in your control. Risk-sharing milestones.
- **Scarcity:** Often none — relies on sales process. Capacity limits work for boutique firms.
- **Name:** Often named after the buyer or outcome. "Enterprise Data Platform Implementation" beats "Data Consulting".
- **Price:** Custom. Proposal-based. Anchored against status quo cost or alternative.

### Common leak: generic capability statement
"We help enterprises transform." Buyer can't picture what they're paying for. Replace with named phases, deliverables, success criteria, and metrics.

## Picking the right format

| If the operator's business is... | Default format | Backup |
|----------------------------------|----------------|--------|
| Solo consultant | Diagnostic → paid engagement | Project-based |
| Freelance specialist | Project-based | Retainer |
| Boutique agency | Hybrid retainer | Project |
| Mid-size agency | Retainer | Performance or hybrid |
| Course creator | Cohort-based | Premium |
| Coach | 1:1 or group | Mastermind |
| Info product creator | Low-ticket self-paced | Mid-ticket cohort |
| Enterprise B2B | POC → annual contract | Implementation project |

The default isn't always right. The operator's specific situation (audience, capacity, fulfillment cost, calendar) drives the final choice.

---

## What breaks format choice

- **Imitating someone else's offer format without their fulfillment capacity.** A $5k mastermind from someone with 200 existing customers isn't the same as launching a $5k mastermind cold.
- **Picking the format that sounds most premium.** A $25k mastermind with 4 buyers isn't more premium than a $2k cohort with 40. Pick what fits.
- **Switching format mid-launch.** Don't pivot from cohort to evergreen mid-promo. Pick, commit, ship.

Match format to capacity. A $500 cohort with 50 buyers is a real business. A $10k mastermind with 2 buyers is a hobby.
