# Pre-Launch Phase — Building Anticipation

The pre-launch phase is where the launch is won or lost. Buyers who arrive at cart-open already convinced convert at 3–5x the rate of buyers who arrive cold. The pre-launch's job is to do the convincing before the cart opens.

**Length:** 7–14 days depending on launch type.
**Goal:** Build anticipation, seed the problem, tease the mechanism, grow the waitlist.

---

## Pre-launch principles

### Deliver real value

Pre-launch emails should teach. Not just tease — teach. The buyer who gets value during pre-launch is the buyer who converts at cart-open.

**Anti-pattern:** pre-launch emails that are 80% "buy buy buy" with 20% content. Buyers tune out. Pre-launch should be 80% value, 20% anticipation-building.

### Open loops

Every pre-launch email should open a loop the cart-open emails close. The buyer stays subscribed because they want the answer.

**Loop types:**
- "On [cart-open date], I'll show you the [mechanism]"
- "There's a specific pattern in pricing that most founders miss — I'll explain it next week"
- "I'm building something for [audience] — if that's you, reply"

### Build the waitlist

The waitlist is the launch's most valuable asset. People on the waitlist convert at 3–10x the rate of cold traffic.

**Waitlist tactics:**
- Early-bird pricing for waitlist (5–10% off, expires at cart-open).
- Early access (waitlist gets the sales page 24 hours before public).
- Bonus for joining (free template, free workshop).

---

## Pre-launch sequence (7-day default)

### Day 1 (T-14) — "Something is coming"

**Goal:** Tease the launch. Start the anticipation loop.

**Email:**
- Subject: "I've been working on something"
- Body: 100–200 words. "I've been building something for [audience]. If [problem], reply 'in' and I'll let you know when it's ready."
- CTA: Reply 'in' / Join the waitlist

**Social post:**
- "Building something. Coming [date]. Reply if you want early access."

---

### Day 2 (T-12) — The problem

**Goal:** Plant the problem deeply. Show you understand the buyer's pain.

**Email:**
- Subject: "The [problem] most [audience] face"
- Body: 300–500 words. Specific problem in buyer's language. Why it's not getting solved by what they've tried. Bridge to "what if there was a different way."
- CTA: Join the waitlist / Reply with your experience

**Social post:**
- Thread on the problem. First tweet/posts gets attention, rest delivers value.

---

### Day 3 (T-10) — The mechanism

**Goal:** Reveal the mechanism behind the upcoming offer. Open the curiosity loop.

**Email:**
- Subject: "The [method name] — what it is"
- Body: 300–500 words. The mechanism, named and explained. Why it's different. "If you want the full thing, [cart opens T-7]."
- CTA: Join the waitlist

**Social post:**
- Mechanism explainer. Carousel or thread.

---

### Day 4 (T-7) — Case study

**Goal:** Add proof. Show the mechanism works.

**Email:**
- Subject: "How [client] did [outcome] in [timeframe]"
- Body: 300–500 words. Case study with specifics. Result + timeframe.
- CTA: Join the waitlist

**Social post:**
- Pull-quote from case study. Visual if possible.

---

### Day 5 (T-5) — Behind the scenes

**Goal:** Build personal connection. Show the work.

**Email:**
- Subject: "Behind the scenes of building [offer name]"
- Body: 200–400 words. What's going into the offer. What you've been working on. Why it matters.
- CTA: Join the waitlist

**Social post:**
- Photo/video of work in progress. "Building."

---

### Day 6 (T-3) — Objection preview

**Goal:** Address the most common objection before it blocks conversion.

**Email:**
- Subject: "You might be thinking..."
- Body: 200–400 words. "You might be thinking [objection]. Here's why that's wrong / what to consider instead."
- CTA: Join the waitlist

**Social post:**
- Address the objection in 1–2 sentences.

---

### Day 7 (T-1) — Cart opens tomorrow

**Goal:** Drive anticipation to specific time.

**Email:**
- Subject: "Doors open tomorrow at [time]"
- Body: 100–250 words. "Tomorrow at [time], cart opens. Set a reminder. Reply 'in' if you're in."
- CTA: Set a reminder / Reply 'in'

**Social post:**
- "Tomorrow. [Time]. [Offer name]."

---

## Waitlist tactics

### Early-bird pricing

Waitlist members get a discount that expires at cart-open (typically 5–10% off).

**Example:** "Join the waitlist by Friday for $500 off the launch price."

**Why it works:** Creates two urgencies — join the waitlist (for the discount) AND convert at cart-open (before the discount expires).

### Early access

Waitlist gets the sales page 24 hours before public cart opens.

**Why it works:** Makes waitlist members feel valued. Gives them a head start.

### Bonus for joining

Offer a free resource for joining the waitlist (template, swipe file, mini-course).

**Example:** "Join the waitlist, get our 50-template cold email swipe file free."

**Why it works:** Builds the list with higher-intent subscribers. They raised their hand.

### Reply-to-confirm

Ask waitlist members to reply with intent ("Reply 'in' if you're in").

**Why it works:** Reply = high intent. Also warms them up for the launch emails.

## Pre-launch content upgrades

Sometimes the pre-launch phase delivers a free workshop or video series that builds the list.

**PLF-style pre-launch (3 videos over 3 days):**
- Video 1: The problem in detail
- Video 2: The mechanism (free preview)
- Video 3: The full method (paid offer tease)

**Why it works:** Each video builds the next. By video 3, the buyer is primed for the offer.

**When to use:** PLF launch type. Mid-to-high ticket offers where the buyer needs substantial setup.

**Anti-pattern:** pre-launch content that gives away the whole method. The free content should open loops, not close them.

---

## Pre-launch list growth

The pre-launch phase is also a list-building opportunity.

**Tactics:**
- Free workshop / webinar as lead magnet (typically 60–90 min).
- Lead magnet related to the offer (template, checklist, swipe file).
- Partner cross-promotions (joint venture partners email their list about your pre-launch).
- Paid ads targeting lookalike audiences.

**Benchmark:** aim to grow the launch list 20–50% during pre-launch. The bigger the launch list, the bigger the launch.

---

## Pre-launch anti-patterns

### Pure hype emails
"Big news coming!" with no content. Buyers tune out.

### All pre-launch, no value
5 emails of "buy buy buy" with no teaching. The launch feels like a sales pitch.

### Fake mechanism reveal
Teasing a "secret method" that doesn't exist. Buyer catches this at cart-open and the launch dies.

### Waitlist that doesn't deliver
Waitlist members get the same emails as everyone else. No early-bird, no early access, no bonus.

### Pre-launch too long
21+ days of pre-launch burns the list. 7–14 days is the sweet spot.

### Pre-launch without social
Email-only pre-launch misses the audience that's on social but not email. Run both.

---

## Quick checklist

Before pre-launch starts:

- [ ] Pre-launch emails drafted (5–7 emails)
- [ ] Social posts drafted (1/day)
- [ ] Waitlist created with early-bird or bonus
- [ ] Cart-open date confirmed
- [ ] Cart-open time locked (specific timezone)
- [ ] Lead magnet or free workshop planned (if using)
- [ ] Partner cross-promo confirmed (if any)
- [ ] List segments ready (active vs. unengaged)
- [ ] Pre-launch content teaches (not just teases)
- [ ] No banned hype words in any pre-launch email
