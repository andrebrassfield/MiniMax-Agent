# Close Cart Phase — Final Push, Last Call

The close-cart phase is the launch's last chance to convert the buyer who's been on the fence. Real scarcity and a real deadline do most of the work. The close-cart emails should feel like the natural end, not a manufactured push.

**Length:** Final 2–3 days of cart-open.
**Goal:** Convert fence-sitters. Close the cart on time.

---

## Close-cart principles

### Real urgency does the work

If the cart has a real deadline and a real cohort cap, the close-cart emails don't need to manufacture urgency. State the deadline. State the cap. Let the buyer decide.

**Anti-pattern:** "Final hours! Last chance! Doors closing!" with no actual deadline or cap. Buyers see through it.

### The buyer who waits isn't ready

Some buyers will wait no matter what. Don't write close-cart emails to convert the buyer who's been told three times. Write to convert the buyer who needed one more nudge.

### One email per day is enough

Daily email during close-cart. Not twice a day. Not three times. Once. The buyer is saturated.

### Stop selling after the cart closes

The cart closes at a specific time. After that, no more launch emails. Switch to post-purchase (for buyers) and waitlist (for non-buyers).

## Close-cart sequence (default 3-email / 3 days)

### Email 1 — 48 hours left (T+1 or T+2)

**Subject:** "48 hours to join [offer name]"

**Body anatomy:**
- Time-bound reminder
- Restate offer (brief)
- Restate guarantee
- "If you've been on the fence, this is the moment"

**Length:** 200–400 words.

**Sample:**

> 48 hours.
>
> The 90-Day MRR Sprint cohort closes Friday at 5pm ET. 3 seats remain.
>
> If you've been watching, here's the offer:
>
> - 12 weekly calls with me, 60 min each
> - Written pricing audit by Day 14
> - Outbound swipe file (50 templates)
> - 30-day execution plan by Week 12
> - 90-day conditional guarantee
>
> $7,500 one-time or 3 × $2,750 (saves $750 with full pay).
>
> Most founders pay $25,000+ for a sales consultant who takes 6 months to ramp. We do this in 90 days for $7,500.
>
> [YES, I'M IN — $7,500]
>
> After Friday, the next cohort opens Q1 2027.

---

### Email 2 — 24 hours left (T+2 or T+3)

**Subject:** "Last day / doors close tomorrow"

**Body anatomy:**
- "Tomorrow at [time], doors close"
- Who the offer is for (and who it isn't)
- Restate guarantee
- "Set a reminder if you're thinking about it"

**Length:** 200–400 words.

**Sample:**

> Tomorrow at 5pm ET, doors close.
>
> The 90-Day MRR Sprint is for B2B SaaS founders at $1k–$10k MRR who want to add $30k+ MRR without hiring a sales team.
>
> It's not for pre-revenue founders. It's not for $50k+ MRR founders. Different stage, different playbook.
>
> If that's you, here's what's left:
>
> - 2 seats in the cohort
> - Same offer: 12 calls, pricing audit, outbound plan, 90-day guarantee
> - $7,500 one-time or 3 × $2,750
>
> [YES, I'M IN — $7,500]
>
> After tomorrow, the next cohort opens Q1 2027.

---

### Email 3 — Doors closing tonight (T+3, morning of close)

**Subject:** "Doors close in [X] hours"

**Body anatomy:**
- Specific closing time
- "If you've been on the fence, this is it"
- Final link

**Length:** 100–250 words.

**Sample:**

> Doors close at 5pm ET today.
>
> 1 seat left in the cohort.
>
> [YES, I'M IN — $7,500]
>
> After today, the next cohort opens Q1 2027.

## Close-cart ads

Push harder on cold traffic during close-cart. Real urgency converts cold traffic better than mid-launch does.

| Day | Ad angle |
|-----|----------|
| T+1 | "Last 48 hours" + outcome |
| T+2 | "Final day" + case study |
| T+3 | "Doors close tonight" + scarcity |

Don't dump the whole ad budget on close-cart day. Spread across the 3 days with the heaviest on T+3 morning.

## What to do when the cart closes

### Stop selling

At cart-close time, stop sending launch emails. The cart is closed. Continuing to sell erodes trust.

### Switch to waitlist for non-buyers

Non-buyers who engaged should get a "join the waitlist for the next cohort" email.

**Sample:**

> Cart closed.
>
> The cohort filled. If you missed it, the next one opens Q1 2027. Join the waitlist for early access + early-bird pricing.
>
> [JOIN THE WAITLIST]

### Switch to post-purchase for buyers

Buyers get the post-purchase sequence (see `/copywriting` post-purchase).

**Sample (first post-purchase email):**

> You're in. Welcome to the 90-Day MRR Sprint.
>
> Here's what to do first: [...]
>
> Your first call is [date]. Calendar invite incoming.

### Tag buyers for follow-up

Tag all buyers in your CRM for:
- Testimonial request (Day 30)
- Referral request (Day 60)
- Re-engagement (Day 180)

## Close-cart anti-patterns

### Extending the cart publicly
"Doors close Friday" → "Actually, doors close Sunday." Once is forgivable. Twice destroys trust. The third time, buyers ignore future deadlines.

### Reopening "one more spot"
"One more buyer, then we're really closing." Each reopen trains buyers that closure isn't real.

### "Last chance" sent weekly
If "last chance" becomes routine, it stops working.

### Continuing to sell after cart closes
Sending launch emails to non-buyers after the cart closed. They didn't buy when it was open; they won't buy now.

### Multiple close-cart emails in one day
"Final hours!" at 9am, 1pm, 5pm. Buyer unsubscribes.

### Manufactured urgency layered on real urgency
Real cohort cap + fake countdown timer. Pick one or the other.

### Close-cart to unengaged list
Subscribers who haven't opened in 90 days shouldn't get close-cart emails. Burns deliverability.

### Empty scarcity
"3 seats left!" when there are 8. Falsifying scarcity erodes trust forever.

## What if the cart doesn't fill?

Some launches don't fill the cohort. Behaviors to consider:

| Situation | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| Cart closes, cohort < 50% full | Waitlist for next cohort. No extension. |
| Cart closes, cohort 50–80% | Fulfill the cohort. Note for next launch: stronger pre-launch. |
| Cart closes, cohort 80%+ | Successful launch. Run again. |
| Cart doesn't close (no enforcement) | Launch failed. Diagnose: offer, copy, list, or scarcity. |

**Don't:** extend the cart to "fill the cohort." This trains buyers to wait and undermines the launch model.

---

## Close-cart list hygiene

After the cart closes, clean the launch list.

**Actions:**
- Tag buyers for post-purchase sequence.
- Tag engaged non-buyers for waitlist + re-engagement.
- Suppress unengaged subscribers (no opens in 90+ days).
- Remove fake/invalid emails that bounced.

A clean launch list converts better on future launches.
