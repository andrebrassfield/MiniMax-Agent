# Mid-Launch Phase — Objections, Proof, Momentum

The mid-launch phase is where the buyers who weren't ready on day 1 get a second chance. Done well, mid-launch adds 30–50% on top of cart-open revenue. Done poorly, the launch coasts and the close-cart phase has to do too much work.

**Length:** Days 2–5 of cart-open.
**Goal:** Handle objections, add proof, maintain momentum.

---

## Mid-launch principles

### Objections are the buyer signal

Buyers who didn't convert on day 1 are usually blocked by a specific objection. The mid-launch emails should surface and address the most common objections.

**Common objections:**
- "Too expensive" (handle with anchor + ROI framing).
- "I don't have time" (handle with effort reduction).
- "Will this work for me?" (handle with matching case studies).
- "I need to think about it" (handle with decision framework + guarantee).
- "What if I don't get results?" (handle with guarantee).

### One objection per email

Don't try to handle five objections in one email. Pick the most common one. Address it directly. Move on.

### Proof beats pitch

Mid-launch is where case studies and testimonials do the most work. The buyer who didn't convert on day 1 needs more evidence that the offer works for someone like them.

### Maintain momentum

Mid-launch is the launch's "valley." Buyers who didn't convert on day 1 have slipped out of urgency mode. Mid-launch emails need to keep the launch feeling alive without manufacturing fake urgency.

## Mid-launch sequence (default 3-email)

### Email 1 — Most common objection (Day 2)

**Subject:** "You might be thinking..."

**Body anatomy:**
- Name the objection in the buyer's words
- Address it directly with specifics
- Bridge to "if that's resolved, here's the link"

**Length:** 300–500 words.

**Sample (handling "too expensive"):**

> You might be thinking: "$7,500 is a lot."
>
> It is. Let's do the math.
>
> If you add $30k MRR in 90 days, your $7,500 pays back in 7 days of new MRR.
>
> Compare that to the alternative: staying at $4k MRR for another 6 months. At your current burn rate, that's 6 months of runway pressure, 6 months of compounding missed revenue ($180k+), and 6 more months of guessing.
>
> The question isn't whether $7,500 is a lot. It's whether 6 more months of stuck is worth avoiding.
>
> 90-day conditional guarantee: complete the work, get the result, or your money back.
>
> [YES, I WANT THE SPRINT — $7,500]

---

### Email 2 — Two more case studies (Day 4)

**Subject:** "Two more quick stories"

**Body anatomy:**
- 2 short case studies with specific results
- Different buyer profiles (show range)
- "If you're in a similar spot..."

**Length:** 300–500 words.

**Sample:**

> Two more quick stories.
>
> **Marcus.** $0 to $8k MRR in 60 days. Pre-launch, Marcus had a $50/mo product nobody was buying. We rebuilt the offer (added a $500/mo tier), the pricing page (moved from monthly to annual), and the close sequence (added a 14-day trial). 60 days later, $8k MRR.
>
> **Priya.** Stuck at $12k MRR for 8 months. We rebuilt her outbound (switched from cold email to LinkedIn warm intros), her pricing (added a $2,500/mo tier), and her close rate (added a 2-call sequence). 12 weeks later, $34k MRR.
>
> Different buyers. Different stages. Same mechanism.
>
> Cohort closes Friday: [YES, I WANT THE SPRINT — $7,500]

---

### Email 3 — FAQ (Day 5)

**Subject:** "Your top 5 questions, answered"

**Body anatomy:**
- 5 FAQ items, each 2–3 sentences
- Mix of objections and logistics
- Bridge to "if your question's not here, reply"

**Length:** 400–600 words.

**Sample FAQ structure:**

> **Q: What if I'm at $500 MRR? Is this for me?**
> A: Probably not. The Sprint is built for B2B SaaS at $1k–$10k MRR. Below $1k, the bottleneck is usually product-market fit, not pricing/outbound. Different stage, different playbook.
>
> **Q: How much time per week?**
> A: 3–5 hours. Calls are 60 min, async is light, homework is specific (not busywork).
>
> **Q: Can I pay in installments?**
> A: Yes. 3 × $2,750 (saves $750 with full pay).
>
> **Q: What if I do the work and don't get results?**
> A: Full refund. Conditional guarantee: complete the calls + homework, don't add at least $15k MRR in 90 days, money back.
>
> **Q: What's the cohort cap?**
> A: 8 founders. Current cohort has 5. 3 remain.
>
> If your question's not here, reply and I'll answer.
>
> Doors close Friday: [YES, I'M IN — $7,500]

## Mid-launch ads

Continue running ads during mid-launch, but adjust creative.

| Day | Ad angle |
|-----|----------|
| Day 2 | Objection-handling angle ("If you've been on the fence...") |
| Day 3 | Case study angle ("How [client] added $40k MRR") |
| Day 4 | Mechanism angle ("The 4-Block Pricing Method") |
| Day 5 | FAQ / final-week angle ("Got questions?") |

Don't dump more budget mid-launch. The cart-close phase is where most cold traffic converts (urgency kicks in).

---

## Mid-launch list hygiene

Mid-launch is a good time to clean the list.

**Actions:**
- Re-engagement sequence to subscribers who haven't opened in 90 days (separate from launch).
- Tag engaged subscribers (opened 3+ launch emails) for follow-up.
- Suppress unengaged subscribers from cart-close emails.

Don't send cart-close emails to unengaged subscribers. It hurts deliverability for everyone.

---

## Mid-launch to close-cart transition

The mid-launch ends and close-cart begins at T+1 (or T+0 for shorter launches).

**Transition signals:**
- Cart-close email queued for T+1 (48 hours left).
- Email 2 of close-cart queued for T+2 (24 hours left).
- Email 3 of close-cart queued for T+3 (doors closing tonight).

The transition should feel like a natural acceleration, not a sudden shift.

---

## Mid-launch anti-patterns

### Same email twice
Sending the cart-open email again as a "mid-launch" email. Buyers recognize the duplicate.

### Pure social proof dump
5 testimonials in a row. One or two specific case studies > five generic quotes.

### Objection handling that introduces doubt
"What if it doesn't work for you?" — never introduce objections the buyer didn't have.

### Mid-launch without sales page updates
The sales page should reflect any new case studies or proof. Update as you go.

### Mid-launch to unengaged list
Subscribers who haven't opened the cart-open emails won't open mid-launch emails either. Suppress them.

### Over-emailing during mid-launch
2–3 mid-launch emails across 4 days. More than that burns the list.

### No mid-launch plan
Launching with only cart-open and cart-close emails leaves 60% of the launch window unused.

## Mid-launch content types

Mix and match across the 3 mid-launch emails:

| Content type | Use for |
|--------------|---------|
| Objection handling | The main objection that blocks conversion |
| Case studies (2) | Adding proof, showing range of buyers |
| FAQ | Logistics, common questions, edge cases |
| Mechanism deep-dive | The "how it actually works" angle |
| Behind the scenes | Personal, less salesy |
| Comparison | Vs. alternatives, vs. doing nothing |

Pick the mix that fits the launch's energy. Objection-handling + case studies is the default.

---

## Mid-launch capacity re-check

By mid-launch, you've converted some buyers. Re-check capacity.

| If you've hit... | Then... |
|------------------|---------|
| <50% of capacity | Continue normally, push harder in close-cart |
| 50–80% of capacity | Continue, mention "filling up" in mid-launch emails |
| >80% of capacity | Pause ads, mention scarcity in mid-launch emails, prep for close |
| 100% of capacity | Stop selling, switch to "join waitlist for next cohort" |

Don't oversell. Better to undersell and offer waitlist than oversell and refund.
