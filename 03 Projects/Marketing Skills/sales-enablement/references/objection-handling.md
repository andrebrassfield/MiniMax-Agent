# Objection Handling — LAER Framework + 12 Common Objections

Objections are not rejection. Objections are the buyer telling you what's blocking the close. The discipline: handle the objection, don't argue with it.

---

## The LAER framework

Adapted from Rackham's objection-handling framework.

**L — Listen:** Let the buyer finish. Don't interrupt. Don't start formulating your response while they're talking.

**A — Acknowledge:** Name the objection. Show you heard it. Don't dismiss or argue.

> "That's a fair concern. A lot of buyers ask that."

**E — Explore:** Ask a question to understand the real objection. The stated objection is often a proxy for the real one.

> "Help me understand — when you say it's too expensive, are you comparing it to a specific alternative, or to your budget?"

**R — Respond:** Address the real objection with specifics. Not generic reassurance.

> "Here's how that works..."

## The 12 most common objections

### 1. "It's too expensive."

**Real objection:** They don't see the value matching the price. Or they don't have the budget.

**LAER response:**

> L: "Got it. Price is a real concern."
> A: "That's a fair one — most buyers we work with start there."
> E: "Help me understand — when you say expensive, are you comparing it to what you'd pay elsewhere, or to your current budget?"
> R: [Based on answer]
>   - If comparing: "The reason the price is $X is [mechanism + components]. The alternative [Y] costs $Z and takes [timeframe]. We deliver [outcome] in [timeframe] for $X. The math works out to [ROI]."
>   - If budget: "What if we structured it as [payment plan]? That brings it to $X/month, which is closer to [comparison]."

---

### 2. "I need to think about it."

**Real objection:** They're not convinced, or they don't want to say no, or they need to check with someone.

**LAER response:**

> L: "Of course."
> A: "I want to make sure you have what you need to decide. Most people who say 'let me think' are missing something specific."
> E: "If you had to decide today, what's the part that would still feel unresolved?"
> R: [Based on answer]
>   - If specific concern: Address it.
>   - If stakeholder: "Who else needs to weigh in? Want to set up a call with them?"
>   - If genuine: "Totally. Let's schedule a 15-min check-in on [specific date]. I'll send a recap of what we discussed."

---

### 3. "I need to talk to my partner/spouse/team."

**Real objection:** They don't have decision authority alone.

**LAER response:**

> L: "Makes sense."
> A: "Most decisions like this involve more than one person."
> E: "What do they typically need to see to feel confident?"
> R: "Let me put together a one-page summary they can review. Want to set up a 15-min call with them next week, or should I send something they can review on their own?"

---

### 4. "I've been burned before."

**Real objection:** They don't trust that this will work. They've paid for similar offers that didn't deliver.

**LAER response:**

> L: "I hear you. That's a real concern."
> A: "Bad experiences are expensive — both in money and time."
> E: "What happened with the last one?"
> R: "Here's how we're different: [specific guarantee]. If we don't deliver, you don't pay. That removes the risk. Plus, [specific case study of a buyer in similar situation who got the result]."

---

### 5. "I don't have time."

**Real objection:** They're overwhelmed, or the effort feels bigger than they can commit to.

**LAER response:**

> L: "Got it."
> A: "Time is the most constrained resource. Most of our buyers are in the same spot."
> E: "What does your week look like for this kind of work?"
> R: "Most buyers spend [X hours/week] on this. The calls are [length], homework is [specific], and the async support is [format]. We've built it to fit around [common buyer schedule]. If you commit [X hours/week], you should hit [outcome] by [timeframe]."

---

### 6. "I'll do it later."

**Real objection:** They don't have urgency. Or they have other priorities.

**LAER response:**

> L: "Totally hear you."
> A: "Timing matters. We want this to work for you."
> E: "What would need to change for this to feel like the right time?"
> R: [Based on answer]
>   - If specific event: "Got it. Let's check back in [timeframe after event]."
>   - If vague: "Most buyers who wait end up paying more in [cost of inaction] than the offer costs. What's the cost of waiting another [timeframe]?"

---

### 7. "I'm not sure it will work for me."

**Real objection:** They don't believe they're the right buyer. Or they've tried similar things that didn't work.

**LAER response:**

> L: "That's the most important question."
> A: "Fit matters. We don't want to take buyers who won't get the result."
> E: "What makes you uncertain?"
> R: [Based on answer]
>   - If specific: Address it.
>   - If general: "Here's who this is for: [specific profile]. If that's you, here's why I'm confident: [matching case study + mechanism explanation]. If it's not you, [different solution] might be a better fit."

---

### 8. "Can you do it for less?"

**Real objection:** They want to negotiate, or they don't have the budget, or they don't see the value.

**LAER response:**

> L: "Appreciate you asking directly."
> A: "We don't discount often, but I want to see if there's a way to make this work."
> E: "If we could get to a price that worked, would you be ready to move forward today?"
> R: [If yes] "What if we did [payment plan]? That brings it to [monthly]. The total is the same, but the cash flow is different."
> R: [If no] "I hear you. The price reflects [mechanism + components]. If we cut [component], it becomes a different offer. I'd rather you make the decision on what we're actually delivering."

**Anti-pattern:** Discounting to close. Discounts train buyers to wait for the next discount.

---

### 9. "I want to check with [other provider] first."

**Real objection:** They're comparison shopping. Or they're stalling.

**LAER response:**

> L: "Makes sense. Comparison is smart."
> A: "Most of our buyers check 2–3 options before deciding."
> E: "What would you be comparing on — price, mechanism, fit, guarantee?"
> R: [Based on answer]
>   - If serious: "Here's how we compare on [dimension]. Most buyers find that [differentiator] is what matters most. Want me to put together a one-pager you can compare against?"
>   - If stalling: "If we deliver [outcome] in [timeframe] for [price] vs. their [alternative], the comparison is [frame]. Want to set up a 15-min call after you've talked to them?"

---

### 10. "I need to see [deliverable] first."

**Real objection:** They don't believe the deliverable will be as good as promised.

**LAER response:**

> L: "Of course — you want to see what you're buying."
> A: "Most buyers want proof."
> E: "What specifically would you want to see?"
> R: [Based on answer]
>   - If specific deliverable: "Here's [anonymized version / sample / case study of similar buyer]."
>   - If general: "Here's how we work: [process]. The first deliverable is [X], due [Y]. If it's not what we promised, you can [exit option]."

---

### 11. "I don't see how this is different from [competitor]."

**Real objection:** They don't see your differentiation. Or they're testing you.

**LAER response:**

> L: "Fair question."
> A: "Most buyers do compare."
> E: "What did you like about [competitor]?"
> R: "Here's what's different about us: [3 specific differentiators]. Most buyers who chose us over [competitor] said the differentiator was [X]."

---

### 12. "Let me get back to you."

**Real objection:** They're not ready. Or they don't want to say no.

**LAER response:**

> L: "Sure."
> A: "I want to respect your time. Most 'let me get back to you' responses mean there's something specific blocking the decision."
> E: "If we could address it right now, would you be ready to move forward? If not, when's a good time to follow up?"
> R: [Lock a specific follow-up date]
>   "Got it. I'll send a recap today, and I'll check back in on [specific date]. If anything changes before then, you have my email."

**Anti-pattern:** Accepting "let me get back to you" without locking a date. The deal dies.

## Objection-handling anti-patterns

### Arguing with the objection
"That's not really a concern because..." Dismissive. The buyer gets defensive.

**Replacement:** Acknowledge. Explore. Respond.

### Generic reassurance
"Don't worry, we'll make it work." Vague. The buyer doesn't trust it.

**Replacement:** Specific. "Here's exactly how it works."

### Pitching harder when they object
The buyer objects. The operator talks faster and louder. The buyer retreats.

**Replacement:** Slow down. Listen more. Address specifically.

### Handling objections out of order
The buyer has 4 objections. The operator tries to handle them all at once.

**Replacement:** One at a time. Address the biggest blocker first.

### Avoiding the objection
The operator changes the subject. The buyer feels dismissed.

**Replacement:** Acknowledge. Address.

### Discounting to overcome objections
Any objection becomes "let me give you 20% off." Trains buyers to wait.

**Replacement:** Address the underlying concern. Discount only as a last resort, and only with conditions.

### "I understand" without exploring
"I totally understand" without asking what they mean. Empty empathy.

**Replacement:** "Help me understand..." Get specific.

## When to walk away

Not every objection can be handled. Some buyers aren't a fit.

**Walk away when:**
- Buyer wants results without effort.
- Buyer wants price below your floor.
- Buyer is comparing on price alone.
- Buyer won't commit to any timeline.
- Buyer's "problem" doesn't match your offer.

**Phrases:**
- "Based on what you've shared, I'm not sure this is the right fit. Here's what I'd recommend instead..."
- "Sounds like [different solution] might serve you better. Want an intro?"
- "We're probably not the right team for this. Let me think about who would be."

Walking away builds reputation. The buyer you referred elsewhere remembers and refers back.

## Quick checklist

Before handling objections on a call:

- [ ] LAER framework internalized
- [ ] Top 5 objections anticipated (based on past calls)
- [ ] Responses for each prepared (with specifics, not generic)
- [ ] Walk-away criteria clear
- [ ] Anchor for the price prepared (in case "too expensive")
- [ ] Guarantee ready to state (in case "I've been burned")
- [ ] Case studies matching buyer profile ready
- [ ] Follow-up date strategy clear (in case "let me think")
