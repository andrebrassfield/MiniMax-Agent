# Follow-Up Sequences — Post-Call + Post-Proposal

The follow-up is where most deals die. Operators send a proposal and wait for the buyer to reply. The buyer gets busy, the proposal goes cold, and the deal evaporates.

The discipline: a systematic follow-up sequence that adds value at each step and locks specific next actions.

---

## Two distinct sequences

### Post-call sequence (after the discovery/sales call)

**Goal:** Lock next steps. Handle "let me think." Move to proposal or close.

**Length:** 2–3 emails over 3–7 days.

### Post-proposal sequence (after sending the proposal)

**Goal:** Convert proposal to close. Handle objections. Lock decision date.

**Length:** 3–5 emails over 14 days.

---

## Post-call sequence

### Email 1 — Recap (send within 2 hours of call)

**Subject:** Great talking today — here's what I heard

**Body:**

> Hey [name],
>
> Great talking today. Here's a quick recap of what I heard and where we landed:
>
> **What you shared:**
> - [Their problem, in their words]
> - [Cost of inaction, in their numbers]
> - [What they want to achieve]
>
> **Where we landed:**
> - [Outcome of the call — next step, proposal, follow-up scheduled, etc.]
>
> **Next step:**
> - [Specific next step with date]
>
> If I got any of that wrong, just reply and let me know.
>
> [Signature]

**Why it matters:** The recap confirms you listened. It gives the buyer something to forward to stakeholders.

**Anti-pattern:** Generic recap that doesn't reference what they said.

---

### Email 2 — Value-add (Day 2–3, only if needed)

**Subject:** Thought this might help — [specific resource]

**Body:**

> Hey [name],
>
> Came across this [article / case study / template] and thought of you. [Specific reason it relates to their situation].
>
> [Link or attached resource]
>
> No pressure on [next step]. Just wanted to pass it along.
>
> [Signature]

**Why it matters:** Adds value without asking for anything. Builds reciprocity.

**Anti-pattern:** Asking for the close in the value-add email. The whole point is no ask.

**Don't send:** If the buyer has already moved to proposal. Save value-adds for stalled deals.

---

### Email 3 — Specific follow-up (Day 5–7, if no reply)

**Subject:** Quick check-in on [next step]

**Body:**

> Hey [name],
>
> Wanted to check in on [next step]. Did you have any questions I can answer? Or if the timing isn't right, no problem — let me know and I'll check back in [timeframe].
>
> [Signature]

**Why it matters:** Locks the decision. "Let me know" is binary — yes or specific timeline.

**Anti-pattern:** "Just checking in" without specifying what you're checking in on.

---

## Post-proposal sequence

The proposal is sent within 24 hours of the call. The post-proposal sequence follows.

### Email 1 — Proposal delivery (Day 0)

**Subject:** Proposal — [offer name] for [buyer name / company]

**Body:**

> Hey [name],
>
> Attached is the proposal we discussed on [date]. Quick summary:
>
> - [Outcome, in their words]
> - [Mechanism, briefly]
> - [Investment, with anchor]
> - [Guarantee, briefly]
>
> Take a look and let me know if you have any questions. If you're ready to move forward, just reply "Yes, let's go" and I'll send the invoice within 24 hours.
>
> The proposal is valid until [date + 14 days].
>
> [Signature]

**Why it matters:** Delivers the proposal with a clear next action. The "valid until" creates gentle urgency.

**Anti-pattern:** Sending the proposal without a next step. The buyer doesn't know what to do.

---

### Email 2 — Check-in (Day 3)

**Subject:** Any questions on the proposal?

**Body:**

> Hey [name],
>
> Checking in on the proposal. Did you have a chance to review? Any questions I can answer?
>
> If you're comparing options or need to loop in a stakeholder, happy to set up a 15-min call: [calendar link].
>
> [Signature]

**Why it matters:** Surfaces objections early. The buyer might be stuck on something specific.

**Anti-pattern:** Just asking "what do you think?" without offering specific help.

---

### Email 3 — Value-add or case study (Day 5–7)

**Subject:** Thought this might help — [case study / resource]

**Body:**

> Hey [name],
>
> Came across this [case study / article / template] and thought of you. [Specific reason it relates to their situation or their stated objections].
>
> [Link or attached]
>
> No pressure on the proposal. Just wanted to pass it along.
>
> [Signature]

**Why it matters:** Adds value. Often re-engages stalled deals.

**Anti-pattern:** Asking for the close. Value-add emails shouldn't have an ask.

---

### Email 4 — Specific objection handling (Day 8–10)

**Subject:** Quick thought on [objection]

**Body:**

> Hey [name],
>
> Wanted to address [specific concern they raised] directly.
>
> [Specific response — 2–3 sentences addressing the concern]
>
> If this resolves it, here's the link to move forward: [link].
>
> If not, let's hop on a 15-min call and talk through it: [calendar link].
>
> [Signature]

**Why it matters:** Most stalled deals are stalled on a specific objection. Addressing it directly unsticks them.

**Anti-pattern:** Generic "let me know if you have any concerns" without addressing the specific one.

---

### Email 5 — Final follow-up (Day 12–14)

**Subject:** Last check-in on [offer name]

**Body:**

> Hey [name],
>
> Wanted to do one final check-in on the proposal. After [date + 14 days], the [early-bird pricing / cohort cap / specific terms] expire.
>
> If now isn't the right time, no problem. If it is, here's the link: [link].
>
> Either way, I'll close out my end of the follow-up. If you want to revisit down the road, just reply.
>
> [Signature]

**Why it matters:** Closes the loop. The buyer knows the follow-up is ending. They can re-engage later without awkwardness.

**Anti-pattern:** Continuing to follow up forever. The buyer starts ignoring.

---

## Follow-up sequence anti-patterns

### "Just checking in"
Vague. No specific question. Buyer ignores.

**Replacement:** Specific check-in. "Any questions on the proposal?" > "Just checking in."

### Asking for the close in every email
Pressure. Buyer retreats.

**Replacement:** Mix of asks and value-adds. Some emails have no ask.

### Continuing forever
Email after email. Buyer unsubscribes or marks as spam.

**Replacement:** Hard stop at Day 14. Re-engage later only with a specific trigger.

### Not handling objections
Buyer raises an objection in reply. Operator sends the next generic email.

**Replacement:** Address the objection directly. Often in a single email.

### Sending the same email twice
Two systems send the recap. Looks unprofessional.

**Replacement:** Dedup systems. Test the welcome flow.

### Generic emails that don't reference the buyer
"Thanks for your time" with no specifics about what they shared.

**Replacement:** Recap in their words. Case studies that match their profile.

### Too many value-adds
Resource after resource. Buyer feels marketed to.

**Replacement:** One value-add per sequence. Make it specific and relevant.

### Following up without locking a date
"I'll check back in next week" — buyer doesn't know what to expect.

**Replacement:** Lock specific dates in the sequence. Buyer can plan around them.

---

## When to stop following up

### Hard stop at Day 14

After 5 emails over 14 days, stop. Don't continue past Day 14.

**Why:** Continuing past 14 days trains the buyer to ignore. It also burns the operator's time.

**If the buyer replies after Day 14:** Re-engage. The sequence resumes.

**If the buyer doesn't reply:** Add to a long-term nurture list. Re-engage in 90 days with a specific trigger.

### Long-term re-engagement (90 days later)

If the buyer was a fit but the timing was wrong, re-engage at Day 90.

**Email:**

> Hey [name],
>
> It's been a few months since we talked about [their stated problem]. Wanted to check in — is [problem] still a priority?
>
> If yes, here's what I'd recommend: [specific offer or approach].
>
> If no, no problem. Just wanted to close the loop.
>
> [Signature]

Don't re-engage without a trigger (their stated problem, a new development in their industry, etc.).

---

## When the buyer goes cold mid-sequence

Sometimes the buyer replies once and then goes silent. Re-engage once, then stop.

**Re-engagement email (after 7 days of silence post-reply):**

> Hey [name],
>
> Following up on [what they asked]. Did you have any other questions?
>
> [Signature]

If no reply after 7 more days, hard stop.

---

## Follow-up tracking

Track every follow-up in the CRM.

**Per buyer, track:**
- Call date.
- Proposal sent date.
- Each follow-up email sent date.
- Buyer replies (and content).
- Buyer status (engaged, evaluating, stalled, closed-won, closed-lost).
- Next action date.

If a buyer goes cold, the CRM tells you when to re-engage.

---

## Follow-up by buyer type

### Engaged buyer (replies within 24 hours)

- Tight sequence (3 emails over 7 days).
- Handle objections directly.
- Lock the close date.

### Evaluating buyer (replies within 3–5 days)

- Standard sequence (5 emails over 14 days).
- Value-add at Day 5–7.
- Handle objections as they arise.

### Stalled buyer (no reply after 5+ days)

- One re-engagement email (Day 7).
- If no reply, hard stop. Add to long-term nurture.

### Cold buyer (no reply from the start)

- Don't follow up past Day 3.
- Add to long-term nurture for 90-day re-engagement.

---

## Quick checklist

Before sending the first follow-up:

- [ ] Recap email drafted (in the buyer's words)
- [ ] Sequence length chosen (based on buyer type)
- [ ] Next-step date locked
- [ ] Value-add resource selected (for Day 5–7)
- [ ] Anticipated objections identified (for Day 8–10)
- [ ] Hard-stop date set (Day 14)
- [ ] Long-term nurture plan for non-repliers
- [ ] CRM tracking set up
- [ ] Test the welcome flow (no duplicate emails)
