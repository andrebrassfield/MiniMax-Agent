# Sales Enablement Anti-Patterns Checklist

The full banned-moves list for sales enablement. A sales process that ships with any of these is weaker, not stronger. Run this list before the sales process goes live.

---

## 1. Pitchy discovery call

**What it looks like:** Operator talks 70%+. Buyer shares a problem. Operator jumps to "we can solve that."

**Why it's banned:** The buyer feels sold, not helped. Doesn't open up. Doesn't close.

**Replacement:** Listen 70%+. SPIN-style questions. Diagnose before prescribing.

---

## 2. "Always be closing" pressure

**What it looks like:** Pushing for the close throughout the call. Operator makes every question a leading question toward the close.

**Why it's banned:** The buyer feels pressured. Retreats. Doesn't refer.

**Replacement:** Closing is a moment, not a vibe. Build to it through diagnose + prescribe.

---

## 3. Fake scarcity on calls

**What it looks like:** "We only take 4 clients a quarter" (when capacity is unlimited).

**Why it's banned:** Buyers can verify. Once they catch it, the operator loses credibility.

**Replacement:** Real capacity. Or no scarcity claim.

---

## 4. Proposal without a call

**What it looks like:** Sending a generic proposal to a cold lead.

**Why it's banned:** The buyer doesn't have context. Doesn't read. Doesn't respond.

**Replacement:** Always have a call first (for high-ticket).

---

## 5. "Just checking in" follow-ups

**What it looks like:** Generic email asking how the buyer is doing. No specific question.

**Why it's banned:** Vague. Buyer ignores.

**Replacement:** Specific check-in. "Any questions on the proposal?" > "Just checking in."

---

## 6. Case studies without specifics

**What it looks like:** "We helped them grow their business" with no numbers, no name, no timeframe.

**Why it's banned:** Doesn't move Perceived Likelihood. Buyer can't see themselves in it.

**Replacement:** Before / After / Bridge. Specific numbers. Specific buyer. Specific mechanism.

---

## 7. Dismissive objection handling

**What it looks like:** "That's not really a concern" or "Most buyers don't worry about that."

**Why it's banned:** Dismisses the buyer's actual concern. The buyer feels unheard.

**Replacement:** Acknowledge. Explore. Respond.

---

## 8. Price without anchoring

**What it looks like:** Quoting the price on the call without a reference cost.

**Why it's banned:** Reads as expensive. Buyer doesn't have a frame.

**Replacement:** Anchor against alternative cost / total value / future cost of inaction.

---

## 9. Multi-call process without value at each stage

**What it looks like:** Dragging the buyer through 3 calls with no progress between them.

**Why it's banned:** Burns the buyer's time. Doesn't build trust.

**Replacement:** Each call should feel like progress. Discovery → Proposal → Close.

---

## 10. Closing attempts before diagnosis

**What it looks like:** Pushing for close within the first 10 minutes. Before understanding the buyer's problem.

**Why it's banned:** The buyer hasn't been heard. Doesn't trust the close.

**Replacement:** Diagnose first. Then prescribe. Then close.

---

## 11. "Let me think about it" without follow-up plan

**What it looks like:** Buyer says "let me think about it." Operator accepts and ends the call.

**Why it's banned:** "Let me think about it" usually means no. The deal dies.

**Replacement:** "What would need to be true for you to feel ready? Let's lock a 15-min call to discuss on [date]."

---

## 12. Generic proposal

**What it looks like:** Same proposal template for every buyer.

**Why it's banned:** Buyer feels like one of many. Doesn't see themselves in it.

**Replacement:** Customize executive summary, situation, cost of inaction, and next steps. Keep the offer components standard.

---

## 13. Proposal without next steps

**What it looks like:** "Let me know what you think" at the end of the proposal.

**Why it's banned:** No clear action. Buyer doesn't know what to do.

**Replacement:** Specific next steps with dates. "Reply 'Yes, let's go' by [date]."

---

## 14. Hidden fees in proposal

**What it looks like:** Proposal says $7,500. Invoice shows $7,800 with "service fees."

**Why it's banned:** Buyer feels tricked. Refund disputes.

**Replacement:** Total price, all-in. Show fees upfront if any.

---

## 15. No guarantee in proposal

**What it looks like:** Proposal doesn't mention the guarantee. Buyer doesn't see risk removal.

**Why it's banned:** Perceived Likelihood stays low. Buyer doesn't close.

**Replacement:** Guarantee stated explicitly in the investment section.

---

## 16. Following up forever

**What it looks like:** Email after email, week after week. Never stopping.

**Why it's banned:** Buyer unsubscribes. Marks as spam. Burns the relationship.

**Replacement:** Hard stop at Day 14. Long-term nurture at Day 90.

---

## 17. Continuing to follow up after they say no

**What it looks like:** Buyer says no. Operator keeps emailing.

**Why it's banned:** Pushy. Doesn't respect the buyer's decision.

**Replacement:** Accept the no. Add to long-term nurture. Re-engage at Day 90 with a specific trigger.

---

## 18. Asking for the close in every follow-up

**What it looks like:** Every email ends with "ready to move forward?"

**Why it's banned:** Pressure. Buyer retreats.

**Replacement:** Mix of asks and value-adds. Some emails have no ask.

---

## 19. Generic case study in proposal

**What it looks like:** Case study that doesn't match the buyer's situation.

**Why it's banned:** Doesn't move Perceived Likelihood. Buyer doesn't see themselves.

**Replacement:** Match case study to buyer profile. Use multiple case studies for different profiles.

---

## 20. Not tracking in CRM

**What it looks like:** Proposals and follow-ups live in email. No central tracking.

**Why it's banned:** Operator forgets who they followed up with. Buyer slips through.

**Replacement:** Track every buyer, every email, every reply, every next-action date in the CRM.

---

## Quick checklist (use before the sales process goes live)

Before the sales process ships, every item must be NO:

- [ ] No pitchy discovery call (70%+ buyer talk time)
- [ ] No "always be closing" pressure
- [ ] No fake scarcity on calls
- [ ] No proposal without a call first
- [ ] No "just checking in" follow-ups
- [ ] No case studies without specifics
- [ ] No dismissive objection handling
- [ ] No price without anchoring
- [ ] No multi-call process without value at each stage
- [ ] No closing attempts before diagnosis
- [ ] No "let me think about it" without follow-up plan
- [ ] No generic proposals
- [ ] No proposal without next steps
- [ ] No hidden fees in proposal
- [ ] No missing guarantee in proposal
- [ ] No following up forever (hard stop at Day 14)
- [ ] No following up after they say no
- [ ] No asking for close in every email
- [ ] No generic case study in proposal
- [ ] No missing CRM tracking

If any item is YES, fix before the next call. Sales anti-patterns erode trust faster than almost any other mistake.
