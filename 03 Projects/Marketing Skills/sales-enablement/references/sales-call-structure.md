# Sales Call Structure — Connect → Qualify → Diagnose → Prescribe → Close

The sales call has a structure. Operators who run calls without a structure rely on charisma — which works until it doesn't. Operators who run calls with a structure close consistently.

This is the structure for a one-call close (30–60 min). Adapt for two-call or multi-call processes.

---

## The 5 sections

### 1. Connect (5 min)

**Goal:** Build rapport. Set the agenda. Lower the buyer's guard.

**What happens:**
- Brief small talk (2–3 min). Match the buyer's energy.
- Set the agenda: "Here's what I'd like to do: 5 min to understand your situation, 15 min to dig into the challenge, 10 min to share how we'd approach it, and 5 min for your questions. Sound good?"
- Confirm time: "We've got 30 min — does that still work?"

**Anti-pattern:** Diving into questions without setting the agenda. The buyer doesn't know what's coming.

**Anti-pattern:** Small talk that goes 10+ minutes. Burns the call.

**Anti-pattern:** Skipping the agenda. The buyer feels like they're being led somewhere.

---

### 2. Qualify (10 min)

**Goal:** Confirm fit. Understand the buyer's situation. Find the problem.

**What happens:**
- 4–6 SPIN-style questions (Situation + Problem).
- Listen for fit signals and mis-fit signals.
- Take notes (or have a note-taker).

**Sample transition:**
> "Let me ask a few questions to understand your situation. Walk me through your current [process]."

**Listen for:**
- Specific, measurable problem (good).
- Vague problem with no specifics (red flag).
- Budget signals (do they ask about price? do they have authority?).
- Timeline signals (when do they need this solved?).

**Anti-pattern:** Asking checklist questions. The buyer feels interrogated.

**Anti-pattern:** Pitching during qualify. The buyer hasn't finished sharing.

**Transition to Diagnose:**
> "Based on what you've shared, let me dig into the implication. If [problem] stays where it is for the next 6 months, what does that cost you?"

---

### 3. Diagnose (10–15 min)

**Goal:** Make the cost of staying stuck vivid. Get the buyer to articulate the value of solving it.

**What happens:**
- 2–3 implication questions.
- 2–3 need-payoff questions.
- Buyer is talking 80%+ of this section.

**Sample flow:**

> "If [problem] stays where it is for the next 6 months, what does that cost you?"
>
> [Buyer answers: "Probably $30k in lost revenue, plus the team morale hit."]
>
> "How is that affecting [other part of business]?"
>
> [Buyer answers]
>
> "If we could solve [problem] in 90 days, what would that be worth?"
>
> [Buyer articulates value]
>
> "Why does solving this matter now?"

**By the end of Diagnose, the buyer has:**
- Articulated the problem in their own words.
- Quantified the cost of staying stuck.
- Articulated the value of solving it.

The buyer has sold themselves. The prescribe phase is now "we deliver what you said you wanted."

**Anti-pattern:** Implying the cost for the buyer. Let them quantify.

**Anti-pattern:** Asking need-payoff questions that lead. Let them articulate the value.

**Transition to Prescribe:**
> "Based on what you've shared, here's how we'd approach this..."

---

### 4. Prescribe (10–15 min)

**Goal:** Present the offer as the mechanism that delivers what the buyer just said they wanted.

**What happens:**
- Restate the buyer's stated problem and desired outcome (in their words).
- Present the offer as the bridge.
- Walk through the six components (from `/offers`).
- State the price with anchor (from `/pricing`).
- State the guarantee.
- Ask if they have questions.

**Sample flow:**

> "So the goal is [buyer's stated outcome, in their words]. The mechanism is [your offer's mechanism]. Here's what we'd do..."
>
> [Walk through six components briefly]
>
> "The investment is $X. For context, [anchor: alternative cost / total value / future cost of inaction]."
>
> "And [guarantee]."

**Don't:** Read the proposal on the call. Send it after. The call is for diagnosis + prescribe, not for documentation.

**Don't:** Over-explain. The buyer has already articulated the value. They're evaluating fit, not learning the offer.

**Don't:** Disclaim the price. State it. Pause. Let the buyer respond.

**Transition to Close:**
> "Based on what you've shared, I think this is a fit. Where do you want to go from here?"

---

### 5. Close (5–10 min)

**Goal:** Get a decision. Not a "maybe" — a yes, no, or specific next step.

**Three possible outcomes:**

#### Yes
The buyer is ready.

> "Great. Here's what happens next: [onboarding step 1] by [date]. I'll send the proposal + invoice today. Any questions before we kick off?"

#### No
The buyer isn't a fit (or isn't ready).

> "Sounds like the timing might not be right. Want me to check back in [timeframe]? Or if [different solution] is a better fit, here's who I'd recommend."

#### Conditional
The buyer needs something to move forward.

> "What would need to be true for you to feel ready to move forward?"
>
> [Buyer states condition]
>
> "Got it. Here's what I can do: [address condition within reason]. Does that work?"

**Anti-pattern:** Pushing for yes when the answer is no. The buyer feels pressured. Doesn't refer.

**Anti-pattern:** Accepting "let me think about it" without a follow-up plan. "Let me think about it" usually means no.

**Anti-pattern:** Long negotiation on the call. The buyer needs time. Move to follow-up.

## Time allocation by call length

| Call length | Connect | Qualify | Diagnose | Prescribe | Close |
|-------------|---------|---------|----------|-----------|-------|
| 30 min | 3 min | 7 min | 8 min | 8 min | 4 min |
| 45 min | 5 min | 10 min | 12 min | 12 min | 6 min |
| 60 min | 5 min | 12 min | 15 min | 18 min | 10 min |

For two-call closes, split at the Diagnose-Prescribe boundary. Call 1 ends after Diagnose. Call 2 is Prescribe + Close.

---

## Buyer talk time

**Target:** 70%+ buyer talk time across the call.

**How to measure:** Estimate. If you've been talking for more than 30 seconds without pause, you're talking too much.

**How to fix:** Use SPIN questions. Paraphrase. Reflect. Summarize. Pause.

---

## Note-taking during the call

Take notes. Capture:
- Buyer's stated problem (their words).
- Buyer's quantified cost.
- Buyer's stated desired outcome.
- Buyer's specific objections or concerns.
- Buyer's decision-making process (who else is involved).
- Buyer's timeline.
- Any personal details (family, location, hobbies) for rapport.

Notes go into the CRM. The post-call email references them.

## Multi-call process

For two-call or multi-call processes, the structure changes:

### Call 1 (Discovery — 30–45 min)
- Connect + Qualify + Diagnose.
- End with: "I'd like to put together a proposal based on what you've shared. Can we get on a quick call next week to walk through it?"

### Between calls
- Send a short follow-up email ("great talking today, here's what I heard...").
- Build the proposal (customized to the buyer's situation).

### Call 2 (Prescribe + Close — 30–45 min)
- Reconnect briefly.
- Walk through the proposal.
- Handle objections.
- Close.

**Don't:** Drag the buyer through calls without value at each stage. Each call should feel like progress.

## Sales call anti-patterns

### Pitching during discovery
The buyer shares their problem. The operator jumps to "we can solve that." The buyer feels pitched.

### Talking more than the buyer
Operator talks 50%+ of the call. Buyer feels sold, not helped.

### Multi-part questions
"What's your timeline, budget, and who's involved?" The buyer doesn't know where to start.

### Skipping diagnosis
Going from "tell me your problem" to "here's our offer." The buyer doesn't feel heard.

### Disclaiming the price
"It's a lot, but..." — never apologize for the price. State it. Anchor it.

### "Always be closing" pressure
Pushing for the close throughout the call. The buyer feels sold.

### Long negotiation on the call
The buyer has 7 objections. Try to handle them all in 5 minutes. Move to follow-up instead.

### Closing without permission
"Let me send you the proposal and the invoice." Without asking if they want to move forward.

### No follow-up plan
"Let me think about it" → no specific follow-up scheduled. The deal dies.

### Fake scarcity on calls
"We only take 4 clients a quarter" (when capacity is unlimited). Buyer can tell.

## Quick checklist

Before running a sales call:

- [ ] Agenda set in the first 5 min
- [ ] 10–15 SPIN questions prepared
- [ ] Time allocations per section (for the call length)
- [ ] Buyer profile reviewed
- [ ] Note-taking template ready
- [ ] Transition phrases prepared
- [ ] Anchor for the price prepared
- [ ] Guarantee ready to state
- [ ] Disqualification phrases prepared (for non-fit buyers)
- [ ] Follow-up email template ready (for post-call)
