# Sales Audit Hook — Template

> **Purpose:** the 3-part script the operator (Andre / Dre Builds) uses in the first cold outreach to a prospect whose local-competitor-auditor brief surfaced ≥3 Tier 1 friction signals. The script is designed to (a) prove the operator did the homework, (b) drop the whitepaper as a credibility artifact, and (c) close on a low-friction ask that does not require the prospect to commit to a sales call.
>
> **Pre-flight required:** the operator must have a `briefs/local-audit-[city]-[niche].md` for the prospect's city + niche, AND the `pov-roadmap.md` for the prospect's business (if they're already a Dre Builds client) OR a fresh ad-hoc audit (if not). Without the audit, this template degrades to generic cold outreach — don't ship it.
>
> **Channel:** DMs (X, LinkedIn, IG), email reply-alls, in-person at industry events. The script is channel-agnostic; the channel dictates the medium-specific format (DMs = 1 message broken into 3 paragraphs; email = subject + body; in-person = 3 sentences).

## The 3-Part Script

### Part 1 — Acknowledge the bottleneck (the homework)

> **Goal:** prove the operator did the audit. Reference a specific friction signal from the prospect's own stack (or city). One sentence. Specific, not flattering.

**Template:**

> Hey [First Name] — I just audited [N] [their niche] in [City], including your [specific page or signal — e.g., "your /contact page" / "your homepage booking flow" / "your Google LSA lead inbox"]. [Specific friction signal in their language, e.g., "The after-hours calls ring to voicemail. No instant booking. No web chat. No service area map."]

**Why this works:** the operator is not selling anything yet. They're showing they did the work. The friction language is the prospect's own language (from the audit), not the operator's editorial spin. If the prospect pushes back ("we have a chat widget"), the operator can say "I saw [X] — does that route to a human after 9pm?" — the homework is the credibility.

**Examples (each grounded in a real friction signal from the Friction Filter taxonomy in the local-competitor-auditor skill):**

- *"I just audited 12 plumbers in Dallas, including your site. The after-hours calls ring to voicemail. No instant booking. No 24/7 web chat. You're leaving money on the table every night."*
- *"I looked at your TikTok Shop storefront. Your LDR is sitting at 6%, you're 2 points over TikTok's 2026 SLA, and the 4% threshold will start costing you $5 per late order in penalties. The settlement hold kicks in at 10%."*
- *"I ran a quick scan on your Google LSA dashboard. You're paying for calls that aren't yours — the wrong service area, the wrong zip code. The dispute flow takes 5 minutes per call. I count ~$X/month in recoverable spend."*

**Anti-patterns to avoid:**

- ❌ "I love what you're doing with [their company]!" — flattery, not homework.
- ❌ "I noticed you might be losing revenue" — vague, no specific signal.
- ❌ "We help SMBs like yours..." — generic sales pitch.
- ❌ Long preamble before the friction — the bottleneck is the hook; lead with it.

### Part 2 — Drop the whitepaper (the credibility artifact)

> **Goal:** show the operator is an engineer, not a snake-oil salesman. Drop the whitepaper as a resource, not a sales asset. One sentence.

**Template:**

> I wrote up the diagnosis in [link to whitepaper / specific section]. The framework is in §2 — the four things a True Agent has to do. The audit math is in §4. Free to read, no opt-in.

**Why this works:** the whitepaper has 3 conflict-of-interest flags at the top, in the exec summary, and at the end of §5. The prospect reading the whitepaper sees the operator saying "this is a marketing document; here's where I'm biased." That's the trust signal. The technical spec is independently verifiable (every claim links to a public URL in the Appendix). The prospect can audit the operator's claims.

**Specific drop lines (pick the one that matches the prospect's vertical):**

- *"I wrote up the diagnosis in the 2026 SMB AI Maturity Report. The Agentic Standard is in §2 — four non-negotiable technical criteria. The math is in §4. COI flags at the top, free to read."*
- *"If you want the technical criteria a 'real' AI agent has to satisfy, §2 of the whitepaper I wrote last week has the 4-criteria test. Section 7.5 also has the 4-week install plan we use."*
- *"Quick read: the 2026 SMB AI Maturity Report. Specifically §3.1 — the ServiceTitan voice agent's own launch limitations. If your vendor is shipping a wrapper, you'll know within 10 minutes."*

**Anti-patterns to avoid:**

- ❌ "Check out our whitepaper!" — salesy, not credible.
- ❌ "We wrote a comprehensive report on..." — corporate voice, not founder voice.
- ❌ Long summary of the whitepaper before the link — the operator is not selling the report; they're sharing the framework.
- ❌ A PDF attached to the DM — the whitepaper is a public Markdown file. Send a link, not an attachment.

### Part 3 — The low-friction CTA (the architecture diagram)

> **Goal:** close on a question that does not require the prospect to commit to a call, a demo, or a free trial. The ask is small. The "yes" path leads to a high-leverage follow-up (a 1-page architecture diagram). The "no" path preserves the relationship for a later touch.

**Template:**

> I audited your stack and found a $[X] revenue leak. I built the fix. Want the architecture diagram? (1 page, no call required.)

**Why this works:** three load-bearing elements.

1. **"I audited your stack"** — the homework is in the past, not the future. The operator is not asking for the prospect's time to do an audit; they already did it.
2. **"found a $[X] revenue leak"** — the number is specific. The X is anchored to the whitepaper's §4 math (e.g., $876K/year for an 8-call/day HVAC shop) or to a client-specific calculation. The prospect cannot verify the exact number in real time, but they can sanity-check it against their own call volume. If the math is in the right ballpark, the credibility is established.
3. **"Want the architecture diagram?"** — the ask is a 1-page document, not a 30-minute call. The prospect can read it on their own time. The "no call required" is the friction remover. The architecture diagram, if read, contains the install plan, the 4-week timeline, and the outcome-pricing commitment — which is the actual sales artifact, delivered in low-friction form.

**CTA variants (pick the one that matches the prospect's psychology):**

- *"I audited your stack and found a $X revenue leak. I built the fix. Want the architecture diagram?"*
- *"Did the math on your missed-call rate. $X/year, by the 4% LDR / $5-late-order math in the whitepaper. I have a 1-page architecture diagram for the install. Want it?"*
- *"Built a 1-page diagram for a 4-week install that closes the gap. No call needed. Want the link?"*

**What the architecture diagram actually is (not in the DM, but the operator must have it ready):**

A 1-page PDF or Markdown file with:
- The 4 Agentic Standard criteria applied to the prospect's stack (which FSM, which e-com platform, which LSA setup).
- A 4-week install timeline (Baseline Audit → Voice Path → Inventory/Ops Path → Outcome Loop).
- The target ROI (the same numbers from the whitepaper's §4, scaled to the prospect's specifics).
- The outcome-pricing commitment (agency bills on booked jobs that complete within 30 days).
- The 5 measurement numbers (per the Forbes/QuickBooks dissection cited in §5.1 of the whitepaper).

**The 1-page diagram is the deliverable.** The DM is the wrapper. The architecture diagram is the actual sales artifact, designed to be self-sufficient (the prospect can decide yes/no without a follow-up call).

## Pre-Flight Checklist (run before sending)

- [ ] Local-audit brief exists for the prospect's city + niche: `briefs/local-audit-[city]-[niche].md`
- [ ] The prospect's business is in the audit (or a fresh ad-hoc audit is queued for the next 24 hours)
- [ ] The friction signal in Part 1 is the prospect's actual friction (not a competitor's)
- [ ] The $[X] in Part 3 is anchored to whitepaper §4 math or to a client-specific calculation
- [ ] The 1-page architecture diagram is drafted and ready to send (not promised and not built)
- [ ] The whitepaper link in Part 2 is the current public URL (not a stale shared link)
- [ ] The DM/email is signed with the operator's name + role, not the agency's brand name
- [ ] The COI flags in the whitepaper are visible at the top of the page (sanity check the URL — the operator doesn't want to send a prospect to a version without the flags)

## Decision Tree (after the prospect responds)

| Response | Operator action |
|---|---|
| "Yes, send the diagram" | Send the 1-page architecture diagram. End the thread. Wait 48 hours. If no response, send a single follow-up: "Did the diagram land? Happy to walk through it on a 15-min call if useful." |
| "Send me more info first" | Send the whitepaper link again. (They may have skimmed it the first time.) Add one sentence: "§2 has the technical criteria; §4 has the math; §5 has the install plan." Wait 72 hours. |
| "Not interested" | Acknowledge, do not push. "Understood. If the missed-call math comes up later, the whitepaper is on the vault." Do not send a follow-up unless the prospect re-engages. |
| "What's your pricing?" | Defer to the architecture diagram. "The diagram has the outcome-pricing model — agency bills on booked jobs that complete within 30 days, not on minutes. The 1-pager explains it." Do not quote a number in the DM. |
| "Who else have you worked with?" | Defer to the agency track record (which is thin — see whitepaper §5.5 caveats). "We just published the technical framework; we're transparent that the agency is new at scale. §5.5 of the whitepaper flags the gap. Happy to share a 2-week pilot structure if you want to be the first audited deployment." |
| "Is this a sales pitch?" | Reframe honestly. "It's a 1-page architecture diagram for a 4-week install. The pitch is the math in §4 of the whitepaper. If the math doesn't work for your stack, no need to talk." |

## Voice Calibration

This template is in the Dre Builds persona's voice. The load-bearing voice rules (per `03 Projects/X-Content-Engine/agents/persona.md`):

- **Staccato period-separation.** 4-5 sentences per DM. Periods, not commas, for impact.
- **Lead with a punch.** The first sentence of Part 1 is the friction signal — concrete, specific, in the prospect's language.
- **Specific numbers.** $[X] in Part 3 is not "a lot" or "significant." It's $876K, or $500/month, or whatever the math says.
- **No fluff.** No "I hope this message finds you well." No "I'd love to chat." No "circling back."
- **No "we" without a receipt.** The Dre Builds persona uses "I" (Andre is the founder). "I audited" not "we audited." Exceptions: the architecture diagram and the whitepaper (which are agency outputs).
- **No emoji, no hashtags, no "DM me to learn more" CTAs.**

## Compliance Notes

- **No login required for any link in this script.** The whitepaper, the local-audit briefs, and the architecture diagram are all public Markdown. Sending a link that requires a login is the fastest way to break the credibility.
- **No public shaming.** The local-audit briefs contain competitor info. The DM names the prospect's own business, not the competitors. Internal use only for the audit; do not publish audit results publicly (would be defamatory).
- **No fabricated numbers.** The $[X] in Part 3 is the whitepaper's §4 math (or client-specific math), not a made-up number. The operator can adjust the [X] to fit the prospect's specifics, but the math must be defensible if the prospect asks "how did you calculate that?"
- **No auto-publish.** The architecture diagram is a one-to-one deliverable. It is not a marketing asset. It is sent to a specific prospect, not blasted to a list.
