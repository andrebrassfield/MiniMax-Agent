# Linguistic Guardrails + Structural Disclaimer Protocol

> The brand operates in a high-scrutiny compliance space. The Gemini displacement strategy research surfaced three non-negotiable compliance requirements specific to CIRS / biotoxin content. This document makes those rules operational and enforceable.

---

## Rule 1 — Prominent medical disclaimer (structural, NOT footer-only)

### The requirement

Every single article, landing page, interactive calculator, and content asset related to CIRS / biotoxin / transcriptomics / GENIE / mold exposure must display a clear, easily readable, and **prominent** medical disclaimer at the TOP of the page.

**Footer-only disclaimers are legally insufficient** under modern FTC guidelines for content that intersects with health decisions.

### The standard disclaimer (use verbatim or substantively equivalent)

> **MEDICAL DISCLAIMER:** This content is for educational and self-help purposes only. The author is not a licensed medical doctor or health provider. Nothing here constitutes medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. The information describes how research tools report observations; it does not tell you what to do about your results. Always consult a qualified healthcare professional before starting, changing, or stopping any supplement, treatment, or protocol. The brand is an educational and comparative resource, not a clinical practice.

### Where the disclaimer appears

| Content type | Disclaimer placement |
|--------------|----------------------|
| Substack post (long-form) | Top of post + footer |
| X thread | First tweet (or pinned reply if length-constrained) |
| LinkedIn post | Top of post + first comment |
| LinkedIn carousel | First slide + final slide |
| Skool pinned post | Top of post |
| Email (any) | Top of body + footer |
| Interactive tool / calculator | Above the input field + below the output |
| YouTube description | Top of description + "show more" expand |
| Podcast episode | First 30 seconds verbal + shownotes top |

### What "prominent" means

- **Visible without scrolling** on desktop
- **Visible above the fold** on mobile
- **Typography clearly distinct** from body text (larger, different color, bordered box)
- **Not hidden** behind "show more" expand, behind paywall, or in fine print

## Rule 2 — Strict linguistic guardrails (clinical → observational)

### The requirement

All CIRS / biotoxin / transcriptomics content must systematically replace clinical, prescriptive language with educational, observational, and self-directed terminology. The brand tells readers what data shows; the brand does not tell readers what to do with the data.

### The conversion table (use this when drafting any CIRS content)

| Prohibited clinical phrasing (HIGH RISK) | Approved observational phrasing (LOW RISK) |
|------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| "To treat your CIRS, you must follow these steps" | "Many individuals managing a CIRS biomarker profile observe improvement when integrating" |
| "This protocol will cure your chronic fatigue and brain fog" | "This framework is designed to support cellular health, mitochondrial function, and systemic resilience" |
| "We recommend taking 4 grams of Cholestyramine twice per day" | "In peer-reviewed literature, the standard clinical dosing utilized under strict medical supervision is" |
| "I diagnose this skin rash as an Actinomycetes colonization" | "Elevated skin-dwelling Actinobacteria metrics on functional environmental panels often correlate with" |
| "You should try the Shoemaker protocol" | "The Shoemaker protocol is one structured clinical approach; alternative practitioners often personalize" |
| "BPC-157 cured my tendinopathy" | "BPC-157 was part of my physician-supervised protocol for tendinopathy; my biomarker data trended in this direction" |
| "This peptide is safe for CIRS patients" | "Some practitioners observe specific response patterns; consult your physician for your specific case" |
| "You need to take X to fix Y" | "Tracking Y over time can show whether interventions are moving the data" |
| "Don't take binders if you have MCAS" | "Many individuals with mast cell hyper-reactivity report variable binder tolerance; the data should guide the conversation with your physician" |
| "The best approach is X" | "Multiple frameworks exist; the framework that works is the one your data shows is moving" |

### Why this matters

The Objective Intent Doctrine treats the brand's content + community + product ecosystem as an integrated system. If the brand publishes content that prescribes specific treatments, the FDA can argue the brand is functioning as a medical provider. Linguistic guardrails are the structural defense against this exposure.

### Self-audit before any CIRS / biotoxin content ships

Before any draft is published, run this 10-question audit:

- [ ] Does the content use any of the "prohibited clinical phrasing" from the table above?
- [ ] If yes, has each instance been converted to the "approved observational phrasing" equivalent?
- [ ] Does the content ever tell the reader to do X (specific action)?
- [ ] Does the content ever tell the reader to take Y (specific compound or dose)?
- [ ] Does the content ever diagnose the reader's condition?
- [ ] Does the content claim any compound is "safe" or "effective"?
- [ ] Does the content promise any outcome (improvement, recovery, healing)?
- [ ] Does the content attack a specific clinical framework (Shoemaker protocol, etc.) by name?
- [ ] Does the content include the prominent medical disclaimer at the top?
- [ ] Does the content's CTA point only to Substack or Skool (no third-party destinations)?

If any answer is "yes" to a question 3-8, the content needs revision before publication.

## Rule 3 — No individualized recommendations

### The requirement

The brand does not provide individualized medical consultations. The brand remains an educational and comparative platform.

### What this means in practice

- **No "based on your results, you should try X"** language
- **No DM interpretations** of individual GENIE reports, CIRS labs, or biomarker panels
- **No Skool comments that recommend** specific interventions to specific members
- **No Substack replies that prescribe** treatment modifications
- **The brand tells readers what data shows**; the protocol decisions are theirs and their physician's

### What the brand DOES provide

- General educational content (this guide, the GENIE Interpretation Guide, etc.)
- Open-access interpretation frameworks (the brand's PCAC framework)
- Standard references to peer-reviewed literature
- Comparative analysis of competing clinical frameworks
- Tools for self-tracking (the upcoming HLA-DR Rosetta Stone calculator, GENIE interpretation framework, environmental testing calculator)
- Redirect to qualified clinicians when readers ask for personal interpretation

### What the brand does NOT provide

- Personalized medical advice
- Dosing protocols
- Diagnosis of individual conditions
- "Treatment" of any condition
- Bypassing the relationship between reader and their physician
- Acting as a clinical practitioner in any capacity

## How these rules apply across content types

### Substack posts

- **Disclaimer placement:** Top of post (above title or below title, before body) + footer
- **Linguistic guardrails:** Apply throughout. No prescriptive language.
- **Individualized recommendations:** None. Even when readers reply with personal situations, redirect to "consult your physician."

### X threads

- **Disclaimer placement:** First tweet (compressed) + pinned reply with full disclaimer
- **Linguistic guardrails:** Apply throughout. The 280-character format makes this harder — default to observational phrasing.
- **Individualized recommendations:** None. Reply language must not prescribe.

### LinkedIn posts

- **Disclaimer placement:** Top of post (above title or as the first 2-3 lines) + first comment
- **Linguistic guardrails:** Apply throughout. Professional context makes this easier — use observational language with operator/researcher framing.
- **Individualized recommendations:** None.

### Skool posts (any tier)

- **Disclaimer placement:** Top of every post (above title)
- **Linguistic guardrails:** Apply throughout. Moderation must remove prescriptive language from member posts too.
- **Individualized recommendations:** None, even in DMs. Brand DMs stay educational.

### Interactive tools (calculators, guides)

- **Disclaimer placement:** Above the input field + below the output
- **Linguistic guardrails:** Apply in all tool descriptions + tool outputs (the tool tells you what your input "may correlate with," not what to do)
- **Individualized recommendations:** None. The tool is observational, not prescriptive.

### Community engagement (Reddit, Facebook groups)

- **Disclaimer placement:** Not applicable to organic community engagement (don't break value-first participation with disclaimer spam)
- **Linguistic guardrails:** Apply in all comments. Value-first participation means dropping frameworks and data that solve real friction; never break character to recommend specific protocols.
- **Individualized recommendations:** None. Comment language must not prescribe even when directly asked. Redirect: "I'm not a doctor; the framework teaches you to read your own data."

## Why these rules exist

The brand operates in a high-scrutiny compliance space because:

1. **The content intersects with health decisions.** Readers make protocol decisions based on what they read. The brand can't claim "we're just sharing information" if the content reads like clinical guidance.

2. **The dominant competitor (survivingmold.com) is heavily paywalled and structured.** When the brand publishes open-access interpretation, the FDA and competitors scrutinize it more. Linguistic guardrails are the structural defense.

3. **The Objective Intent Doctrine applies.** Brand content + community + product ecosystem = integrated system. If any component prescribes, the entire ecosystem can be characterized as medical practice.

4. **The "I'm not a doctor" disclaimer alone is insufficient.** FTC guidance and case law have established that disclaimer-only defense fails when the content itself reads as clinical guidance. Linguistic guardrails + prominent disclaimers + observational framing is the multi-layered defense.
