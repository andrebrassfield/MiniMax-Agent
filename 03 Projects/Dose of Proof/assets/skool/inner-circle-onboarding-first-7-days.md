## DAY 2 — The 5 Biomarkers Intro (Educational Post)

**Post type:** Community channel
**Post title:** The 5 biomarkers I track daily — and why the standard panel misses them

---

**Body:**

When I was in the worst of it, my standard bloodwork kept coming back "normal."

My allergist looked at histamine. My rheumatologist looked at ESR/CRP. My primary care looked at basic CBC. None of them caught the upstream drivers.

Here's the 5-marker floor I built for myself — the minimum data set that actually catches the system. The PDF in the resources channel walks through how to test for each one and what to ask your doctor for.

**1. Morning HRV (Heart Rate Variability)**
Your vagus nerve's daily report card. Free phone app (HRV4Training, Elite HRV, or Whoop if you have one). Track first thing in the morning — before caffeine, before screens, before checking email. The number moves when the upstream driver is being addressed.

When my upper cervical instability was flaring: HRV was chaotic in the low 30s-40s. After Blair adjustments + terrain work: consistent mid-50s+. The trend matters more than any single day's number.

**2. TyTron Paraspinal Thermal Pattern**
Most people have never heard of this. It's an autonomic infrared scan that upper cervical specialists use — measures the thermal pattern along the paraspinal muscles. Asymmetrical or wavy patterns indicate nerve interference at specific spinal levels.

Direct visual feedback on whether the mechanical driver is being addressed. Not every upper cervical specialist has a TyTron — if yours doesn't, ask what they use instead. If your specialist doesn't have any autonomic measurement tool, that's a signal to find a different one.

**3. Tryptase + Urine MCAS Mediators**
Baseline tryptase is often normal in MCAS. The real signal comes from catching it during or right after a flare — plus urine markers (N-methylhistamine, LTE4, prostaglandin D2 metabolites).

These need to be ordered during a flare window. Talk to your doctor about the protocol — most standard labs (Quest, LabCorp) can run the urine panel, and timing matters more than the baseline draw.

**4. ESR + CRP + Symptoms Tracking**
Standard inflammatory markers + a daily "guarding score" (1-10) for how stuck your skin and muscles feel + flushing/heat episodes per day.

When the upstream driver stabilizes, both the blood markers and the subjective guarding score drop together. This is how you connect the mechanical fix to the downstream inflammation you actually feel.

**5. Vitamin D + Magnesium Status**
Low Vitamin D and magnesium are extremely common in this presentation. They make everything worse — ligament laxity, mast cell reactivity, vagal tone, muscle guarding.

Once these were in range, my nervous system became noticeably less reactive and I held adjustments longer. Terrain foundation. Get these optimized first.

---

**The pattern:**

These 5 aren't a complete picture. They're the floor — the minimum data set that catches the upstream drivers most doctors miss. If your numbers look fine on all 5, you can rule out a chunk of the terrain. If they're off in specific patterns, you know where to look upstream.

## DAY 4 — Vagus-Neck Mechanics Basics (Educational Post)

**Post type:** Community channel
**Post title:** Vagus + neck mechanics — what the connection actually is (without the wellness hype)

---

**Body:**

There's a lot of "vagus nerve" content out there. Most of it is wellness hype. Here's what the connection actually looks like in my own data — without crossing into prescription territory.

**The anatomy, simply:**

The vagus nerve runs from the brainstem down through the neck, past the carotid arteries, into the chest and gut. It's the body's main parasympathetic highway — the brake on the sympathetic "fight or flight" system.

At the upper neck (C1-C2, atlas and axis), the vagus nerve passes within millimeters of the vertebral arteries and the cervical sympathetic chain. When the upper cervical spine is unstable — even subtly — the mechanical irritation can affect vagal signaling.

That's the connection. Not mystical. Mechanical.

**What the data shows in my case:**

When my upper cervical instability was flaring:

- HRV was chaotic (low 30s-40s, high day-to-day variability)
- Resting heart rate was elevated (75-85 vs my baseline 60s)
- The "skin stuck" guarding pattern was constant
- Mast cell flares correlated with neck flare days, not with food or environmental triggers

When the upper neck stabilized (Blair upper cervical adjustments, posture work, vagal breathing):

- HRV stabilized (consistent mid-50s+)
- Resting heart rate dropped back to my baseline
- Guarding pattern softened
- Mast cell flares became shorter and less intense

I'm not telling you your vagus nerve is unstable. I'm not telling you to get your neck adjusted. I'm telling you what the data looked like in my case, and what upstream driver turned out to be the most actionable.

**What this means for your framework:**

If you're tracking HRV and it's chaotic, ask: what's the most upstream mechanical driver I can identify with data? Sometimes the answer is structural. Sometimes it's nutritional. Sometimes it's both.

**Three ways to start measuring vagal tone (no equipment):**

1. **Morning HRV** — free phone app, before caffeine, before screens
2. **Vagal breathing test** — inhale 4 seconds, exhale 6 seconds, 10 cycles. If your HR goes up during inhale and down during exhale, vagal tone is responsive. If it doesn't change, the brake may be off.
3. **Gargle test** — time how long you can gargle continuously. Longer = better vagal tone to the throat muscles. Crude but directional.

These are not diagnostics. They're data points. Bring them to your physician and ask what they mean in your specific case.

## DAY 6 — Biomarker Tracking Basics (Educational Post)

**Post type:** Community channel
**Post title:** How to actually track biomarkers — the daily protocol

---

**Body:**

Most people who try to "track their biomarkers" give up after 2 weeks. Here's the protocol that actually works — the one I built for myself, then formalized for the framework.

**The 4-daily-track protocol (10 minutes total):**

**1. Morning HRV (2 minutes)**
- Free app: HRV4Training, Elite HRV, or Whoop
- First thing in the morning, before caffeine, before screens
- Sit or lie still for 60 seconds, then run a 60-second reading
- Log the number + the standard deviation (variability)
- Trend over weeks matters more than any single day

**2. Guarding Score (30 seconds)**
- 1-10 scale: how stuck do your skin and muscles feel right now?
- 1 = completely soft, no guarding
- 10 = constant full-body clench
- Log at the same time each day (morning works best)
- Track alongside any flares (heat, flushing, brain fog)

**3. Flushing + Heat Episodes (30 seconds)**
- Count: how many episodes today?
- Severity: 1-10 each
- Trigger if obvious (food, stress, environment, sleep)
- The pattern emerges over weeks, not days

**4. Sleep + Mental Clarity (2 minutes, end of day)**
- Sleep hours + quality (1-10)
- Mental clarity at end of day (1-10)
- One sentence: what was different today?

**The 4-weekly-track protocol:**

- Sunday: review the week's trends. What moved? What didn't?
- Adjust nothing for 2 weeks minimum — most interventions need 14-21 days to show in the data
- At 30 days: zoom out. What 30-day pattern is emerging?
- At 90 days: change the approach if the data hasn't moved

---

**The tool I use:**

A simple spreadsheet. Columns: date, HRV, HRV-SD, guarding, flushes, flush-severity, sleep hours, sleep quality, clarity, one-line note. Rows: every day.

No app needed. No subscription. The data is yours.

When Dose Calc launches (Q4 2026), there'll be a tracking module — but the spreadsheet works today. The framework doesn't require the tool.
