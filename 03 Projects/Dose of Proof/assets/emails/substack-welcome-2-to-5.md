### Body

Hey [First Name],

Quick definition first.

**PCAC** stands for two things at once. The FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee — the body meeting July 23-24 to decide which peptides move into legal compounding categories. And my framework: **Proof-Centered Approach to Craniocervical + Autoimmune Chaos.**

Both matter. Same acronym, two meanings. I'll always specify which one I'm talking about.

---

**The framework, in plain language.**

PCAC is a way of thinking about chronic, multi-system illness that replaces "Symptom Whack-a-Mole" — where each specialist treats one mediator while missing the upstream driver.

Four parts:

**1. Map the terrain, not just the symptoms.**
Instead of chasing histamine, or cortisol, or a single inflammatory marker, I track the system. Vagal tone (HRV). Mechanical instability (cervical imaging + TyTron scans). Mast cell activity (tryptase + urine mediators). Standard inflammation (ESR/CRP). Foundation nutrients (Vitamin D, magnesium).

The five biomarkers aren't a complete picture. They're the floor — the minimum data set that catches the upstream drivers most doctors miss.

**2. Prove it works or change the approach.**
Every intervention gets a baseline, a tracking protocol, and an honest 30-90 day review. If the numbers don't move, I change the approach. No "give it 6 months and see." No sunk-cost loyalty to a protocol that isn't moving the data.

**3. Treat the upstream, not the downstream.**
The mast cells are firing because the vagus brake is off because the neck is unstable. Treating mast cells directly without addressing the neck is treating the symptom of the symptom of the symptom. The framework asks: what's the most upstream driver I can identify with data? Treat that first.

**4. Stay proof-centered, never prescriptive.**
This is where PCAC the framework and "my body, not yours" intersect. I tell you what I'm doing in my own recovery. I never tell you what to do in yours. Your physician, your biomarkers, your decisions.

---

**What the framework is NOT.**

- Not a protocol you buy in a box.
- Not a dosing guide.
- Not a supplement stack recommendation.
- Not a substitute for working with a physician who understands your terrain.

**What it IS.**

- A way to read your own data honestly.
- A way to tell whether an intervention is actually working.
- A way to refuse the fragmented specialist treadmill.
- A way to ask better questions of your care team.

---

**The math side: reconstitution.**

Because most of the biohacking community uses volumetric dilution for peptides, the framework includes three formulas:

- Concentration: C = (vial mass in mg × 1000) ÷ BAC volume in mL = mcg/mL
- Draw volume: V_draw = (target dose in mcg) ÷ C
- Tick math: ticks = V_draw × 100 (for 1mL syringe with 100 ticks = 1mL)

The math is the math. It doesn't change with regulatory decisions. The Dose Calc SaaS (Q4 2026) operationalizes these formulas into a tool — but the math is teachable in a notes app today.

**Position:** Day 7 after opt-in

### Body

Hey [First Name],

One week in. Here's the operational shift.

**Symptom Whack-a-Mole** is what happens when you have a multi-system illness and see one specialist per system. The allergist treats the histamine. The rheumatologist treats the joint pain. The psychiatrist treats the anxiety. The gastroenterologist treats the gut. Each one prescribes. None of them connect the dots.

The patient takes 4-6 medications, gets 4-6 partial explanations, and is still sick.

This is what I lived through for 7 months. It's what most people in this community have lived through.

**Terrain mapping** is the alternative.

Instead of starting with symptoms and working outward, you start with the system and work inward.

Step 1: Pull the 5 biomarkers (HRV, TyTron scan if available, tryptase + urine MCAS mediators, ESR/CRP + symptom log, Vitamin D + magnesium). Most can be ordered by your primary care. Some require a specialist willing to order outside the standard panel.

Step 2: Build a daily tracking log. Morning HRV (free phone app, before caffeine, before screens). Guarding score (1-10, how stuck your skin and muscles feel). Flushing episodes per day. Sleep hours + quality. Mental clarity (1-10). The log is for trend, not perfection.

Step 3: At 30 days, look for patterns. What moves together? What moves independently? Where are the upstream drivers showing up downstream?

Step 4: At 90 days, change the approach if the numbers haven't moved. No loyalty to a protocol that isn't producing data.

---

**The shift that changed everything for me:**

I stopped asking "what's wrong with me" and started asking "what's the most upstream thing I can measure, and is it moving?"

That question reframes every specialist visit. You stop being a passenger in someone else's diagnosis. You become the operator of your own data.

---

**Three things that make this work:**

1. **You need a physician who respects the data.** Not all do. If yours dismisses HRV or symptom logs as "not real medicine," you need a different physician — not a different framework.

2. **You need to log daily, even when you feel like shit.** The data on bad days is the data that shows the pattern. Skipping logging when you flare is the fastest way to lose the thread.

3. **You need a 90-day window before you judge.** Healing is not linear. Biomarkers trend over months, not days. The framework requires patience most people don't have — which is exactly why it works for the people who do.
