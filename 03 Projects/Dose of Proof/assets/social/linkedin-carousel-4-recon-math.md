## Slide 2 — THE 3 FORMULAS

**Body:**

**Formula 1 — Concentration:**
C = (Mass × 1000) / Volume
(mcg/mL)

**Formula 2 — Required Draw:**
V_draw = Target Dose / C
(mL)

**Formula 3 — Syringe Ticks:**
Ticks = V_draw × 100
(U-100 insulin syringe)

That's it. Three formulas. No mysticism.

---

## Slide 3 — THE DANGEROUS DEFAULT

**Headline:** The default most calculators suggest

**Body:**
- 10mg vial
- 1mL BAC water (the standard)
- Target dose: 250mcg

C = (10 × 1000) / 1 = 10,000 mcg/mL
V_draw = 250 / 10,000 = 0.025mL
Ticks = 0.025 × 100 = **2.5 ticks** ❌

---

## Slide 4 — WHY 2.5 TICKS IS UNSAFE

**Headline:** The naked-eye problem

**Body:**
- 2.5 ticks = 0.025mL
- That's the space between the two smallest marks on a U-100 syringe
- A microscopic slip of the thumb = double the dose
- Receptor saturation curves are non-linear

I learned this the hard way.

---

## Slide 5 — THE FIX

**Headline:** Change the dilution, not the dose

**Body:**
Add 2mL BAC water instead of 1mL:

C = (10 × 1000) / 2 = 5,000 mcg/mL
V_draw = 250 / 5,000 = 0.05mL
Ticks = 0.05 × 100 = **5 ticks** ✓ SAFE

Same dose. Measurable draw.

---

## Slide 6 — THE SAFETY PRINCIPLE

**Headline:** Always aim for ≥5 ticks

**Body:**
- If your math produces <5 ticks → dilute more BAC water
- The math doesn't change. The dilution does.
- The dose stays the same.
- The safety improves dramatically.
