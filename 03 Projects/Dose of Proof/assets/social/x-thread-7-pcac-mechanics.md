---

**Tweet 2:**

Step 1: Someone nominates a bulk substance for compounding.

Anyone can nominate — a doctor, a compounder, a researcher, an advocacy group.

The nomination has to include safety data, manufacturing info, and a proposed clinical use.

BPC-157 was nominated years ago. So were KPV, TB-500, MOTs-C, Semax.

---

**Tweet 3:**

Step 2: FDA reviews the data.

The FDA looks at:
- Animal toxicology
- Human case reports (often anecdotal for peptides)
- Manufacturing quality (sterility, purity, consistency)
- Immunogenicity risk
- Whether the nomination is for a legitimate medical indication

---

**Tweet 4:**

Step 3: FDA assigns a Category.

**Category 1** = compounder may produce for human use (pending conditions)
**Category 2** = too much safety risk, compounding prohibited
**Category 3** = insufficient data, more info needed

BPC-157 is currently **Category 2** because of immunogenicity concerns + insufficient characterization.

---

**Tweet 5:**

Step 4: The Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) reviews.

The PCAC is an external expert committee that makes non-binding recommendations to the FDA.

The FDA can follow or ignore the recommendation. The committee's vote is advisory, not decisive.
