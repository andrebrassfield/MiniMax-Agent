**Tweet 2:**

Background.

BPC-157 is a 15-amino-acid synthetic peptide derived from human gastric juice. It has been studied in animal models for angiogenic, regenerative, and anti-inflammatory effects.

It is NOT an FDA-approved drug for any indication. It is not in Category 1 (which would permit compounding). It has been a Category 2 bulk substance since 2023.

---

**Tweet 3:**

What Category 2 actually means:

— Cannot be used as a bulk substance in compounding under Section 503A
— "Significant safety risks" — the FDA's language
— Specifically cited for BPC-157: immunogenicity risk + insufficient API characterization + impurity profile unclear

These are manufacturing + safety concerns, not efficacy findings.

---

**Tweet 4:**

In April 2026 the FDA REMOVED BPC-157 from the Category 2 list.

That's a meaningful procedural change. It doesn't approve the compound. It opens the door for the PCAC to formally evaluate it for inclusion on the 503A Bulks List.

The July 23 meeting is that evaluation.

---

**Tweet 5:**

What the committee actually does on July 23:

The PCAC reviews clinical and safety data for nominated bulk substances and votes on whether to recommend inclusion on the 503A Bulks List.

A "yes" vote is a recommendation. It is NOT a final FDA decision. The FDA then considers the recommendation and issues its own determination — sometimes months later.

---

**Tweet 6:**

What a positive recommendation would change:

— State-licensed 503A compounding pharmacies could theoretically prepare BPC-157 formulations for individual patients with valid prescriptions
— Physicians could prescribe it within the compounding pathway
— Telehealth platforms partnered with 503A pharmacies (Marek Health, Lifeforce) could offer it under physician oversight

This is physician-supervised only. Not direct-to-consumer.

---

**Tweet 7:**

What a positive recommendation would NOT change:

— BPC-157 would still not be FDA-approved for any indication
— Manufacturers could not sell it direct-to-consumer
— "Research only" marketing that names human therapeutic uses would still violate 21 CFR 201.128
— Sourcing from gray-market vendors would still be illegal

The map changes the legal pathway. It does not change the underlying architecture.
