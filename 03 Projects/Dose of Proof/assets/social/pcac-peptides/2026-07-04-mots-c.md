**Tweet 2:**

Background.

MOTs-C is a mitochondrial-derived peptide (MDP) encoded in the mitochondrial genome. It's been studied for metabolic regulation, insulin sensitivity, and bone density in animal models.

It's a real molecule. The mechanism is interesting. The cultural moment around it is loud.

---

**Tweet 3:**

The GLP-1 context:

The popularity of semaglutide and tirzepatide has made every metabolic peptide a "is this the next GLP-1?" story. MOTs-C is no exception.

This is the trap: hype migrates from one mechanism to another faster than the regulatory process can catch up.

---

**Tweet 4:**

Regulatory state:

MOTs-C was on the Category 2 bulk substances list and was removed in April 2026 — same as the other three compounds in this PCAC batch.

The FDA's Category 2 concerns: manufacturing + safety characterization, not efficacy findings.

---

**Tweet 5:**

**Indication #1 — Obesity.**

The PCAC will review available data on MOTs-C for the obesity indication — metabolic regulation, body composition, weight-related outcomes.

The committee may vote on obesity separately from the osteoporosis indication, or bundle both votes. Watch the published PCAC agenda to see how the votes are structured.

---

**Tweet 6:**

**Indication #2 — Osteoporosis.**

Same compound, same docket day, different indication. The PCAC will separately review MOTs-C for bone-density and osteoporosis-related outcomes.

The evidentiary bar is identical to obesity: indication-specific data, not mechanism enthusiasm.

---

**Tweet 7:**

**Voting structure (procedural note).**

Two indications in one docket entry is unusual. The committee can vote them separately or as one bundled item. The published PCAC meeting agenda will confirm which.

Both votes are non-binding recommendations to the FDA. The agency then issues its own determination on 503A Bulks List inclusion — sometimes months later.

---

**Tweet 8:**

The data-versus-hype line:

Animal data on MOTs-C and metabolic regulation is suggestive. Human data is limited.

What you can do as a reader: separate "the mechanism is interesting" from "this compound is approved for X." Those statements are years of regulatory work apart.
