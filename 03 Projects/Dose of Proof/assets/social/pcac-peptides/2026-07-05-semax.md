**Tweet 2:**

Background.

Semax is a synthetic heptapeptide analog of ACTH (4-10). It's been studied in Russian and European literature for neuroprotective, nootropic, and cerebrovascular effects.

It is NOT FDA-approved. It is not in Category 1. The mechanism class (CNS-active peptides) carries additional regulatory considerations.

---

**Tweet 3:**

Regulatory state:

Semax was on the Category 2 bulk substances list and was removed in April 2026 — same procedural move as the July 23 batch (BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTs-C).

The Category 2 concerns: manufacturing + safety characterization. The CNS-active mechanism adds neurotoxicology and abuse-liability questions to the safety review.

---

**Tweet 4:**

What's actually being evaluated July 24:

The PCAC will review available data on Semax for cerebral ischemia indication (reduced blood flow to the brain, often post-stroke) and vote on whether to recommend inclusion on the 503A Bulks List.

Note: cerebral ischemia is a serious medical condition. The committee will weigh efficacy data, not just safety.

---

**Tweet 5:**

The non-binding nature applies here too:

PCAC "yes" = recommendation to FDA. FDA then considers and issues its own determination on 503A Bulks List inclusion. Months between recommendation and final action.

The procedural framework is identical to the July 23 batch. The committee composition may differ slightly.
