---

**Tweet 2:**

Setup:

You run a Substack or YouTube channel about peptides. Educational. Compliant language. Top of funnel.

You run a Skool or Discord community. Members discuss dosing, sourcing, side effects.

You link the community from your content. Members know where to buy.

You don't sell anything yourself. You don't link your storefront.

That still reads as integrated.

---

**Tweet 3:**

The FDA's framing:

— Educational content that drives traffic to a community
— Community that hosts dosing discussion + sourcing referrals
— Operator that profits from the community (paid tier, affiliate revenue, brand deals)

= A single integrated marketing operation for an unapproved drug.

---

**Tweet 4:**

Moderation isn't the defense it sounds like.

Removing a single dosing thread after-the-fact doesn't undo the ecosystem pattern. The agency weighs:
— how the community is positioned
— what topics recur
— whether operators curate or just remove the worst offenders
— whether the operator profits from the community's existence

---

**Tweet 5:**

The architecture of the trap:

Education (top of funnel) → Community (engagement + retention) → Affiliate or storefront (monetization)

This is the standard creator economy stack. It is also the exact pattern the FDA reads as integrated marketing of an unapproved new drug when the topic is research-restricted compounds.

---

**Tweet 6:**

The separation rule:

The brand operator's money and the brand operator's editorial content must be clearly separated from any dosing, sourcing, or efficacy discussion.

That means: no community you operate. No Discord you moderate. No Skool with paid tiers where dosing is discussed. No affiliate link to a vendor inside the same ecosystem as your educational content.

Or it means: very strict separation, with legal review.

---

**Tweet 7:**

This is the architectural reason Dose of Proof has no integrated community. Yet.

The plan: Skool launches AFTER the July 23-24 PCAC decision window, with explicit topic boundaries (no dosing discussion in the community; physician-only dosing conversations happen elsewhere). Until then, the only community surface is the Substack comment thread — moderated, single-layer, no paid tier.

Architecture before content. Always.
