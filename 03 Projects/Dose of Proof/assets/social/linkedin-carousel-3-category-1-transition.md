## Slide 3 — WHAT CHANGES (Consumers)

**Body:**
- 503A compounding pharmacies can legally produce the compound
- Telehealth platforms (operating within the law) can offer it through supervised care
- Board-certified physicians can prescribe it through their practice
- Supply chain through legitimate channels expands
- Pricing may decrease as more manufacturers enter the market

---

## Slide 4 — WHAT DOESN'T CHANGE (Consumers)

**Body:**
- The math (reconstitution math doesn't change)
- The biomarker framework (HRV, TyTron, tryptase, ESR/CRP)
- The need for physician supervision
- The FDA review timeline (Category 1 is "compounding permitted pending conditions" — not "unrestricted")
- The Objective Intent Doctrine (sourcing language still creates legal exposure)

---

## Slide 5 — WHAT CHANGES (Prescribers)

**Body:**
- Functional medicine doctors can prescribe through their standard channels
- Compounding pharmacies can fulfill prescriptions legally
- Telehealth platforms can offer supervised care without legal gray-zone exposure
- Insurance reimbursement becomes possible for some use cases
- Documentation requirements remain strict (FDA still tracks outcomes)

---

## Slide 6 — WHAT DOESN'T CHANGE (Prescribers)

**Body:**
- Physician-patient relationship still required
- Off-label prescribing still allowed (but with documented risk discussion)
- Patient education still operator's responsibility (not the pharmacy's)
- Outcome tracking still operator's responsibility
- FDA still tracks adverse event reports
