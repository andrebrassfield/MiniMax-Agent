---

**Tweet 2:**

The math:

**C = (Mass × 1000) / Volume**

10mg vial, 1mL BAC: C = (10 × 1000) / 1 = 10,000 mcg/mL

**V_draw = Target Dose / C**

250mcg / 10,000 mcg/mL = 0.025mL

**Ticks = V_draw × 100**

0.025 × 100 = 2.5 ticks.

---

**Tweet 3:**

2.5 ticks = 0.025mL = the space between two of the smallest marks on a U-100 syringe.

At that scale, a microscopic slip of the thumb = double the dose.

I've been there. I learned the hard way.

---

**Tweet 4:**

The fix: change the BAC water volume, not the dose.

Add 2mL of BAC water instead of 1mL.

Re-run the math:
- C = (10 × 1000) / 2 = 5,000 mcg/mL
- V_draw = 250 / 5,000 = 0.05mL
- Ticks = 0.05 × 100 = 5 ticks

5 ticks is measurable. The dose is the same.

---

**Tweet 5:**

The principle: pick a BAC water volume that produces a draw of AT LEAST 5 ticks on your syringe.

If the math produces <5 ticks, dilute more.

The math doesn't change. The dilution does.
