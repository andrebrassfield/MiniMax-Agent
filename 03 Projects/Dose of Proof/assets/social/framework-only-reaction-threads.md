## Thread B — "What I'm Watching For in Any PCAC Committee Vote"

**Position:** Posts immediately after Thread A on Day 1 (Jul 23), OR pre-loaded for Day 2 (Jul 24) if needed
**Format:** 5 tweets

---

**Tweet 1 (hook):**

When the FDA's PCAC committee makes a recommendation, most people watch the headline.

Here's what I watch instead — the 4 signals that actually matter for the framework lens:

🧵 What to look for in the committee's reasoning, beyond the category call:

---

**Tweet 2:**

Signal 1: The committee's specific reasoning.

Did they cite immunogenicity concerns? Manufacturing data gaps? Insufficient human trials? Or did they cite a substantive safety issue?

The reasoning reveals what the committee would need to see for a future category change. That's the lead indicator.

---

**Tweet 3:**

Signal 2: Whether they asked for additional data.

A recommendation to "consider additional data" or "reconvene in 12 months" signals a future transition possibility.

A flat "Category 2 confirmed" with no path forward signals the compound is stuck.

The language matters.

---

**Tweet 4:**

Signal 3: Any committee dissent.

If 1-2 committee members dissent publicly (in the meeting minutes or press release), that signals political pressure on either side.

- Dissent toward more restrictive: industry/safety advocates pushing for caution
- Dissent toward more permissive: patients/physicians pushing for access

The dissent is the signal about where the next review might land.

---

**Tweet 5 (CTA):**

Signal 4: The FDA's framing in the post-meeting press release.

The FDA often reframes committee recommendations in their own language. The committee says one thing; the FDA press release says another.

I'll translate each in real time on Substack within 4 hours of any committee vote.
