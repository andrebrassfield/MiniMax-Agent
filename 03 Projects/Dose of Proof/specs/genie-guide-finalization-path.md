## Gate 1 — Placement of "What the brand is NOT doing" subsection

### Recommendation: **Add as a new Section 10 — "What Dose of Proof is NOT doing (with this guide and the GENIE test)"**

### Rationale

**Why not Section 9 (Compliance + linguistic guardrails)?**
- Section 9 is meta — it's about HOW the brand communicates (linguistic rules, prominent disclaimer requirement, "My body not yours")
- A "what we're not doing" subsection is more specific — it scopes the brand's actual relationship to the GENIE test
- Mixing them creates a 6-section compliance section, which dilutes the meta-rules

**Why not a footnote?**
- Footnote signals "low importance" — but the "what we're not doing" framing is structurally important for the brand's compliance posture
- The Gemini displacement research specifically identified that "we don't sell kits" / "we don't provide interpretation services" framing is what makes the educational positioning credible
- It deserves a numbered section so readers can reference it

**Why Section 10 (new section)?**
- It's the LAST section readers see, right before the closing CTA
- It pre-empts the most common reader question: "Can you interpret my GENIE results?" / "Do you sell test kits?"
- It frames the brand's positioning cleanly: educational + observational + upstream

### Proposed Section 10 content (drafted below)

> **What Dose of Proof is NOT doing (with this guide and the GENIE test)**
>
> The brand's relationship to the GENIE test is strictly educational. Specific scope:
>
> **The brand does NOT sell GENIE test kits.** If you're considering getting a GENIE test, you'll work with your physician and the lab that runs the panel. The brand doesn't accept orders, doesn't bundle kits, doesn't have an affiliate relationship with the lab.
>
> **The brand does NOT provide interpretation services for individual GENIE reports.** This guide is a decoder for how the report is structured. It doesn't tell you what your specific pattern means, what to do about it, or whether your results are "good" or "bad." Those conversations are between you and your qualified clinician.
>
> **The brand does NOT recommend specific protocols based on GENIE findings.** The framework is observational. It doesn't tell you which supplements to take, which treatments to pursue, or which lifestyle changes to make. Your physician interprets your specific results in the context of your specific case.
>
> **The brand does NOT position itself as a clinical practice.** The author is not a licensed medical doctor. The brand is an educational and comparative resource, not a clinical service.
>
> **The brand does NOT comment on specific clinical decisions made by your physician.** Your physician has access to your full case. The brand has access to its own data. The brand's role is to narrate its own recovery process with biomarker data — not to second-guess yours.
>
> **What the brand IS doing:** tracking its own biomarker data (HRV, TyTron, GENIE, serum C4a / TGF-beta-1 / MMP-9), publishing repeat test patterns, narrating the recovery process in observational terms, and letting readers find the Substack if they want to follow along.
>
> The framework is upstream. The protocol decisions are yours and your physician's. "My body, not yours."

### Estimated added length: ~250 words.

## Gate 3 — Length recommendation (4,200 → 5,000 words?)

### Recommendation: **YES, expand to 5,000 words via a citations pass — but schedule it for the FINAL-READY cycle, not before Section 10 is added**

### Rationale

**Why expand to 5,000?**
- Gemini research target was 5,000 words. Current draft is ~4,200. Gap = 800 words.
- Adding citations INCREASES word count naturally (peer-reviewed references + brief excerpts)
- Citations increase search credibility — Google ranks content with primary sources higher
- The 90-day roadmap Phase 1 success criteria includes "ranks for 'how to read genie test report' within 30 days" — citations support this
- Dre explicitly said "aim to move the full guide to FINAL-READY in the next 1-2 cycles" — citations are the next 1-2 cycles' work

**Why not expand before adding Section 10?**
- Section 10 is a structural addition (250 words + new section)
- If we expand to 5,000 first then add Section 10, we end up at 5,250 words
- If we add Section 10 first (4,450 words) then expand to 5,000 via citations (550 more words), we hit the target cleanly
- Order: Section 10 → citations pass → final read-through

**Why not expand to 7,000+ words?**
- 5,000 is the Gemini research sweet spot for "comprehensive but not overwhelming"
- Going beyond 5,000 risks reader fatigue — long-form guides hit a cliff at 5,000-6,000 words for first-time readers
- The guide's value is in being USEFUL for first-time readers, not in being exhaustive
