The reaction-discipline ask has NO Mavis-executable option — the reaction IS the operator's embodied stance on the article, and the cron can't write an operator's stance. So the "ask once, then decide" pattern from cron-discipline.md §1 doesn't apply; what's needed is a different rule (relax the threshold to 14d, OR add a special ping for cycle-bound articles, OR acknowledge the cron can't enforce embodied provenance and stop pretending). **What reading all three reveals:** The reaction-discipline cycle is a **structurally different** failure mode than the cron-discipline (ask-once) cycle. The cron-discipline cycle (FB-Engine AM/PM) has a closing path: enumerate options → ask → wait → execute pre-authorized terminal step (delete). The reaction-discipline cycle has NO closing path on the Mavis side because the artifact being produced (the reaction) is *defined* as the operator's embodied stance. The cron can detect missing reactions and move the article; it cannot write the operator's stance. The "if I have to ask you for something twice, you failed" discipline from `cron-discipline.md §1` doesn't apply because the Mavis-side "second ask" doesn't produce the operator's reaction any more than the first did. The cycle is *not* an ask-once problem; it's a structural unfixable asymmetry.

The non-obvious finding: **the 2026-06-23 connection's "embodied provenance" principle predicts this failure mode.** If skills/copy/loops require operator judgment to have integrity, then *any* cron layer that depends on operator-produced artifacts has an unfixable asymmetry on the Mavis side. The cron can detect, escalate, and surface; it cannot execute. The reaction-discipline cycle is the visible symptom; the asymmetry is the cause. The mitigation options for the reaction case are NOT "Mavis-execute-the-decision" — they are:
- (A) Relax the threshold (7d → 14d) to reduce cron frequency — buys time but doesn't fix the asymmetry.
- (B) Special ping when dwell time exceeds threshold — surfaces the asymmetry without closing it.
- (C) Stop pretending and acknowledge the asymmetry in `REACTION-RULE.md` — codifies that reactions require operator judgment, not cron enforcement.
- (D) Allow Mavis to write a "Mavis draft reaction" as a placeholder — produces volume but violates embodied provenance (the Mavis-side draft is by definition not the operator's stance).

The 2026-06-25 morning synthesis proposes option A (relax to 14d). It does NOT enumerate options B/C/D. That gap is the same shape as the 06-24 `ships-that-arent-load-bearing` connection — a documented rule that names the symptom but doesn't enumerate the closing options.

The 2026-06-23 connection said: "Skills built from imagined practice are just worse agents." Today's corollary: **cron cycles built without acknowledging the embodied-provenance asymmetry are just vaults with a fancy index.** The reaction-discipline cycle is the cron-side example. The fix is not to make the cron smarter (it can't write the reaction); the fix is to acknowledge the asymmetry and design the cron to surface it, not close it.

This is Thesis 2 (a second self is active reasoning, not good capture) in the sharpest form: a Mavis that pretends to enforce embodied provenance via a cron cycle is performing capture, not reasoning. The reasoning happens when the cron *names* the asymmetry and proposes structural mitigations (A/B/C/D above). Today's morning synthesis did step 1 (name the cycle); it didn't do step 2 (enumerate mitigations). That's the gap this connection fills.

This is also Thesis 4 (long-term knowledge in vault, not always-on context) in the form that explains *why* the asymmetry is unfixable on the Mavis side: the operator's embodied stance IS the long-term knowledge the vault holds. Reactions aren't "captures of what's in the article" — they're "the operator's stance on what's in the article, grounded in the operator's lived experience." That lived experience is not in any Mavis-accessible artifact; it's in the operator. The cron can move the article; it cannot move the stance.
