# Connection: Dose of Proof's "My Body, Not Yours" rule ↔ Loop Engineering's "you are the loop"

**Why this connection matters:** The Dose of Proof brand voice file mandates a single high-stakes rule: "Never say 'You should take BPC-157 for your gut.' Say 'When my gut was destroyed, I looked into the data on BPC-157...'" The Loop Engineering article names a single high-stakes insight: "Two people can build the exact same loop and get completely opposite results. One uses it to move faster on work they understand deeply. The other uses it to avoid understanding the work at all. The loop does not know the difference. You do." Read in isolation, the first is a copywriting discipline and the second is a meta-engineering observation. Read together, they are the same principle applied to two different surfaces: **the operator's embodied understanding is what gives the artifact (copy / loop) its integrity, and no amount of format craft can substitute for that.**

- Claim: The article's load-bearing closing insight: the loop is a tool, but the operator's judgment is what gives it direction. A loop designed by someone who deeply understands the work moves it forward; a loop designed by someone avoiding understanding just produces faster drift. The discipline of "you are the loop" — the system runs but YOU decide — is what separates the two.

**What reading both reveals:** Both notes name **embodied vs. prescribed knowledge** as the load-bearing distinction. Dre's rule forces every piece of copy through the embodied filter: it must trace back to a specific lived experience, with specific scans / labs / protocols attached, framed as "what I did" rather than "what you should do." The Loop Engineering insight forces every agent loop through the equivalent filter: it must trace back to the operator's deep understanding of the work, with explicit goal / stop-condition / verification-gate attached, framed as "what the system does under my judgment" rather than "what the system does on its own."

The parallel is sharper than surface-level: **both notes treat the format (copy / loop) as a vehicle, not a substitute.** A beautiful sales page written by someone without lived experience is hollow; a beautiful loop designed by someone who doesn't understand the work is also hollow. The FDA, the audience, and the loop's downstream behavior can each detect the hollow version — but only if the verification step checks *embodied provenance*, not surface coherence.

For Mavis specifically, this collapses to a discipline: **when building a skill (the Mavis analog of a personal-brand voice or an agent loop), audit it for embodied provenance.** A skill that says "use this checklist when launching a product" is hollow if the operator (Mavis) doesn't actually know what a good launch looks like in this niche. A skill that says "always start with the lived protocol before recommending anything" has embodied provenance because the operator (Dre) has the lived protocol. The thesis implication: **Skills beat agents because skills can encode embodied knowledge procedurally, but only if the skill was built by someone who actually has the embodied knowledge to encode.** A skill built from imagined practice is just a worse agent.
